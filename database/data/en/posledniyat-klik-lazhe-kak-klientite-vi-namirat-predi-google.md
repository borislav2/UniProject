The Last Click Lies and How Customers Find You Before Google
the-last-click-lies-how-customers-find-you
An order is credited to the last click, but the path began elsewhere, in TikTok, Meta or a chat with ChatGPT. How to read reports without being fooled.
---
The report says that the order came from Google. So you put more money into Google. But the customer may have seen your product on TikTok three weeks ago, compared prices in ChatGPT yesterday and only today typed your name into the search box.

Stanislav Vladimirov, a Digital Marketer at Netpeak Bulgaria, spoke at Digital Community Day 2026 about exactly this. The last click is only part of the path.

## Three steps called SEE, THINK and DO

Vladimirov uses a model with three stages. In SEE the customer sees you for the first time. In THINK they compare and judge. In DO they buy or send an enquiry.

Presence begins before the till, he says. In practice, Meta and TikTok are most often the first meeting. ChatGPT is the comparison, a conversation with an adviser. Google and Bing are the final click.

The business sees one order. The platforms may each count it separately, so the sum of the channel reports is often bigger than the real sales.

## What this looks like for a small business

Here is an example we made up for illustration. A beauty salon in Ruse posts a short video on TikTok. A customer watches it and does nothing. A week later she asks ChatGPT where to get a manicure in Ruse and how much it costs, and sees the name of the salon. Then she opens Google Maps, reads the reviews and calls. The salon's report says that the call came from the Google profile. The first two contacts are not visible at all.

If the salon decides from the report that TikTok does not work, it will stop exactly the thing that brought the customer. That is why Vladimirov insists on thinking about the path and not about the last stop.

## What the examples show

The numbers come from the speaker's own projects. The clients are not named, so read them as an illustration and not as proof.

For a second hand electronics shop on TikTok, ROAS was 7.15 in GA4, revenue 84,368 euro, spend 11,794.99 euro and cost per click 0.10 euro. Here the cheap click leads to sales.

For ChatGPT Ads across six projects the results were mixed. A glasses shop had 1,204 clicks and no conversions for 1,534.82 euro. A B2B agency got 17 actions for 449.51 euro. A tyre shop made 7 purchases for 2,667.70 euro. An air conditioning company got 2 orders for 288.85 euro. CPM was between 7.37 and 17.05 euro. Vladimirov also shows the opposite case and says a cheap click does not guarantee a sale, because the glasses shop had many clicks and not one conversion.

The speaker also shows an example with 96.6 percent higher revenue at 22.6 percent higher ad spend, comparing October 2025 with October 2026, but the channels and the client are not named. We do not use it as an argument.

Both kinds of examples say the same thing. A channel is neither good nor bad by itself. What matters is what it is asked to do, what it costs and whether you measure it correctly.

## Formats in ChatGPT

Ads in ChatGPT are made with a product feed, with one or several images. Context hints do not guarantee that an ad is shown. In other words, this is a new channel with little experience and not a sure profit.

## What a small business should do

The speaker proposes three changes, which are creatives (AI and real faces), simpler accounts and clear roles for the channels. He adds one framework, which is that the new media complement Google and Meta and do not replace them.

**First, set up measurement before you launch a new channel.** In GA4 you need at least events for an enquiry, a call and a purchase. Without them you do not know what worked.

**Second, give each channel a role.** Meta and TikTok are for the first sighting, while search engines and the Google profile are for the final choice. Do not measure them with the same yardstick. A channel that shows you to new people will not give the most last clicks, but without it there is nobody to convince later.

**Third, test small and with a clear aim.** The speaker's advice is a small test with a clear goal, deadline and budget. For example, a set sum, four weeks and a target number of enquiries. If the goal is not reached, you stop and write down what you learned.

**Fourth, ask your customers.** The cheapest report is one field in the form that asks where they heard about you. That also shows the channels that clicks do not show.

**Fifth, explain the differences in the report.** When platforms show more orders than the shop has, it is not a mistake but a different way of counting. It is better to know that in advance than to decide on wrong numbers.

## In the end

Do not switch off a channel just because the report shows few last clicks. And do not add a new one just because it is fashionable. First measurement, then a small test, then a decision.
