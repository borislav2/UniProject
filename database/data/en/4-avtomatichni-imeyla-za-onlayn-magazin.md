The Four Automatic Emails Every Online Shop Needs
four-automatic-emails-every-online-shop-needs
Welcome, abandoned cart, viewed product and after purchase emails. You set them up once, and they work for you every single day.
---
Holiday campaigns get noticed, but the money in email marketing often comes from flows, which are automatic emails that start by themselves when a customer does something. At eCommerce Annual Summit 2026, Mihail Atanasov of Z-Consult explained which ones are the main ones and how to build them properly.

Their advantage is that you set them up once. After that they work every day, for every customer, at the right moment, without anyone remembering to send them.

## Where subscribers come from

A pop up for new visitors is shown only to people who are not yet subscribed, and it offers something in return for an email, such as a discount, a gift or a useful guide. A good pop up collects an email from at least 5 percent of the people who see it. What works is a big close button, one clear field and an offer that fits your average price.

A second prompt on the product page is for people who closed the pop up but stay on a product for at least 30 seconds or are about to leave. It offers the same deal.

### How not to annoy people

Do not show the pop up the moment someone opens the site. Let them see what you sell first. Do not show it again to someone who closed it. On a phone the window must not cover the whole screen without a clear close button, because Google dislikes such windows and people dislike them even more. And the consent box for marketing emails cannot be ticked in advance.

## 1. The welcome email

It starts right after the person subscribes. It delivers the promised code, introduces who you are, shows what the products are for, adds customer reviews and reminds people that the offer expires. Do not stretch it over time, because interest is highest in the first days.

### A sample sequence

Immediately, send thanks and the promised code with a button to your best sellers. After one or two days, explain who you are, where the products come from and what sets you apart, with a story or two or some reviews. After three or four days, remind them that the code expires if unused. If the person buys in the meantime, the remaining emails of the series stop and they move to the after purchase flow.

## 2. The abandoned cart

The person reached the order and stopped. These emails answer their objections, such as delivery, returns and warranty, and show exactly the products they left behind. A discount is possible, but **not at once**. Use it only in the third email, otherwise you teach customers to wait for a reduction.

### Why people leave the cart

The most common reasons are practical. An unexpected delivery cost, a long delivery time, a complicated registration, a missing preferred way to pay, or simply that the person was interrupted. That is why the first email is often only a reminder, and the following ones answer the questions.

About an hour later, send "You left something in your cart" with a photo of the product and a button back. The next day, cover delivery, returns, warranty and product reviews. After two or three days, if you have one, offer a small incentive such as free delivery or a code.

The abandoned cart works only if the customer left their email before stopping. That is why it is good for the email field to come first in the order form.

## 3. The viewed product

This is for people who looked at a product but did not reach the cart. It shows the viewed product with its benefits. It is important that it does not overlap with the other emails, otherwise the customer gets three messages a day.

This email works only for subscribers whom the email program recognises on the site. That makes it weaker than the abandoned cart, but it still brings sales, especially for expensive products that people consider for longer.

## 4. After purchase

This is the most important one for bringing customers back. It thanks the customer, explains how to use the product, suggests suitable additions, reminds them when it is time to refill and collects a review. Unlike the others, it is good to spread this one over 30, 60 and 90 days, matched to the delivery.

### A sample sequence

After delivery, send thanks, how to use the product and whom to ask if there is a question. After one or two weeks, ask for a review, when the customer has already tried the product. After about a month, suggest a suitable addition to what they bought. And when the product usually runs out, send a reminder to refill. The timing depends on the product, because a face cream runs out in a month or two, while shoes are bought once a year.

## How to keep them from overlapping

If all the flows start at once, one person can receive a welcome, an abandoned cart and a viewed product email on the same day. A rule that helps is that a purchase stops the welcome, the cart and the viewed product emails, that the abandoned cart takes priority over the viewed product, and that no person receives more than one automatic email a day. Most email programs, including Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Brevo and Omnisend, have a setting for this.

## What about campaigns

The most profitable campaigns are three. A new product, a voucher for a set amount, even as a plain text email, and a big sale once every three or four months, such as the brand's birthday or a summer Black Friday.

## How to tell whether they work

For each flow watch mainly one number, which is **how many sales it brought**. Email programs show it directly when they are connected to the shop. Opens are useful but no longer precise, because some mail apps count them automatically.

If a flow brings little, look at where it loses people. It may be the subject line, with few opens, the content, with few clicks, or the site, with clicks but no purchases.

Write down the five most common hesitations customers have before buying and the five reasons they buy again. That is the content of the four emails. If you want us to set them up for you, see [email marketing and automations](/en/services/email-marketing).
