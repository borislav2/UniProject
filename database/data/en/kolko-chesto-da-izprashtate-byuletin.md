How Often to Send a Newsletter Without Annoying People
how-often-to-send-a-newsletter
There is no universal newsletter frequency. What matters is what you promised people, what you have to say and how consistently you can write.
---
Imagine an accountant from Varna who hesitates between two extremes. If he sends a newsletter every day, he fears people will unsubscribe. If he sends it once every six months, he fears nobody will remember him. Both fears are justified, and the right answer depends mostly on what you promised people when they signed up.

## There is no magic number

However hard you search, there is no frequency that is right for everyone. A cafe may send a weekly offer, because its customers expect something new. An accountant makes sense writing once a month, around deadlines for declarations and changes in the law. A car service might write twice a year, before winter and before summer, when people really think about tyres and air conditioning.

So instead of looking for somebody else's figure, begin with the question of how often you have something useful to say. If in a given month you have nothing to say, it is better not to write than to fill the email with general talk.

## The promise at sign up is the rule

The most reliable guide is what people read when they left their email. If under the form it says they will receive a monthly newsletter, sending three emails a week breaks the agreement, even if no law forbids it. People sign up with a certain expectation, and when it is broken, the easiest answer is to unsubscribe or, worse, to mark you as spam.

Marking as spam does more harm than unsubscribing. When several people do it, mail providers start to regard your emails as suspicious, and they begin to land in junk even for people who otherwise want to read them. So it is better to make unsubscribing easy to see than to hide it.

## Consistency matters more than frequency

A newsletter that comes out on the first Tuesday of every month becomes a habit. The reader knows when to expect it and recognises it in their inbox. A newsletter that comes out three times in two weeks and then vanishes for four months is seen as something random, and after such a long gap many no longer remember why they signed up.

So it is sensible to start with a frequency you can really keep up. A monthly email that always goes out is better than a weekly one that stops after the third issue. You can always increase it when you see that you have the material and that people read it.

## What the people themselves tell you

The best way to learn whether you write too often is to see how readers react. If after each send more people than usual unsubscribe, or some reply that they are overloaded, that is a sign to slow down. If they open and click and nobody complains, you have room to write a little more often.

It also helps not to rely only on your feeling. If you put one email on a sheet of paper in front of you and ask yourself whether you would read it with interest, the answer often becomes clear before you press the button.

Do not be afraid of a few unsubscribes. A person who does not want to receive your emails would not buy from you, and so your list is left with people who really are interested. A small and careful list gives better results than a large but indifferent one.

## Different frequency for different emails

Not every email is a newsletter. A booking confirmation, an appointment reminder at the dentist and an invoice are useful messages that the customer expects, and they do not count in the same category. They should be sent when needed and should not be mixed with advertising.

An advertising offer is another matter. If you have a seasonal offer, for example a discount on a winter car check, one email on the occasion is fully justified and does not damage trust as long as it is not repeated constantly. Think also about letting people choose how often they receive emails from you, for example only the important news or everything.

## What to do from here

Write on a sheet what you promised at sign up, how much material you really have during the year and how many hours you can spend. From these three things a sensible frequency will come out, and it will not be invented by anyone else. Then watch the unsubscribes and the replies for three to four months and adjust.
