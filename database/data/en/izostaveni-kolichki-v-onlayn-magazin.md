Abandoned Carts in an Online Shop and What to Check
abandoned-carts-in-an-online-shop
Why customers leave a full cart and do not pay. What to check first in the order, delivery and payment steps of your shop.
---
The most frustrating thing in an online shop is a customer who chose a product, put it in the cart and then walked away just before paying. They have already got past the hardest part, which is finding you and believing you. If you lose them here, small corrections often bring back more people than more advertising.

## First look at what the order looks like

Before you search for complicated reasons, place a real order from a phone. Not from your computer, but from a phone, on a slow connection, without knowing how the shop works. Count how many steps, how many fields and how many times you feel like giving up.

This simple test often reveals problems nobody on the team sees, because everyone is used to them. A small homemade sweets shop in Plovdiv may discover that the payment button disappears under the keyboard, or that the form asks for the same address three times.

## Hidden costs are not forgiven

One of the most common reasons for an abandoned cart is an unpleasant surprise at the end. The customer saw the price of the product, and at the last step a delivery fee appears that they did not expect. That feeling of being cheated is strong, and many people close the page at once.

So show the delivery price earlier. A short line on the product page or in the cart is enough, with a clear statement of what it costs and from what sum it is free, if you have such a threshold. People accept even an expensive delivery when they know it in advance. The same goes for any other fee, such as packaging or cash on delivery, which is better shown in time than appearing at the last moment.

## The fewer fields the better

Every additional field is a reason for someone to give up. Ask only for what you need in order to send the parcel. Do you really need a date of birth, a second phone number or a long description?

Do not make the customer create an account before buying. Many shops demand registration before payment, and that is an obstacle for someone who wants to buy once and leave. Offer purchase as a guest, and leave registration as an option after the order.

Think about automatic address filling and the right keyboard on the phone too. If the phone field shows letters instead of digits, that is a small inconvenience that adds up.

## Ways to pay and to receive

People pay in different ways, and if something they expect is missing, they often leave. In Bulgaria many buyers prefer cash on delivery, and others pay by card. Show clearly which options you have, before the last step.

The same applies to delivery. If the customer wants to receive the parcel at a courier office and you offer only delivery to an address, or the other way round, some of them will give up. Ask what your customers use instead of guessing.

Check how clearly it says when the order will arrive. A vague "within a few days" creates uncertainty, while a concrete time removes it.

## Trust and speed at the moment of payment

When people enter personal data, they look for signs of security. Clear return terms, a visible phone number and address, a real company name and a working site without errors create trust. A page with a problem at the most important moment, such as a strange looking form or an error message, destroys it at once.

Check that the site opens over a secure connection and that there are no error messages at payment. If one of the payment methods sometimes fails, customers will not write to you, they will simply not buy.

If the cart page loads slowly or the button does not respond at once, the customer begins to doubt whether the payment went through. It is not rare for a person to press twice and get a double order, which is even worse.

Speed is part of trust, so check how the shop behaves on an average phone. Large photos that have not been reduced and unnecessary plugins are a frequent cause of slowness.

## What to do first

Do not try to fix everything at once, because then you will not know what helped. Start with one thing, for example showing the delivery price, and watch for several weeks whether anything changes. Then move on to the next. If possible, ask the customers who gave up as well, by sending a short polite email, because their answer is more valuable than any guess.
