Reminder Emails That Bring Back Customers Who Went Quiet
reminder-emails
The easiest customer to win is one who already knows you. Here are four kinds of automatic reminders that work for salons, car services and clinics.
---
Many customers do not leave because they are unhappy. They simply forget. Their lives are full and you are not the first thing on their mind. One timely reminder is often enough.

Reminder emails are among the most useful automatic messages, because they help both sides. The customer does not miss something important, and you do not lose a customer who was happy with you.

## 1. A reminder based on time

This suits services that repeat after a certain period. A car service can write "Twelve months have passed since your last oil change. Shall we book you in?" A dental practice can write "It is time for your check-up and cleaning." A salon can write "Six weeks have passed since your last colouring." A vet can write "The annual vaccination for Rex is coming up." An accountant can write "The deadline for submitting the declaration is at the end of the month." The message goes out automatically when a set time has passed since the last visit.

Here is an example. The subject line reads "Ivan, it is time for an oil change". The message says "Hello Ivan, last October we changed the oil on your Golf. It is usually time for a new change after a year or 15,000 kilometres, whichever comes first. If you like, book a convenient day by calling us or just reply to this email with a day and a time. Best wishes, Peter, Auto Sever."

Notice that the message says when the last visit was, why you are writing now and exactly how the person can book.

## 2. A seasonal reminder

A car service can remind people to change tyres in autumn and spring. A roofing company can offer an inspection before winter. A restaurant can announce a festive menu and December bookings. An air conditioning company can offer servicing before the summer. A beauty salon can suggest treatments that suit the autumn after the summer.

Seasonal emails go to all your customers at once, at the right moment. It is best to send them a little before the season, while there are still free appointments.

## 3. The "we have not seen you for a while" email

This is for customers who have not been for several months. Keep it short, personal and without pressure. "Hello Ivan! We have not seen you for a while. If your car needs anything, we are here."

How long is a while depends on the business. For a hairdresser it may be three months, for a car service a year and a half. Think about how often your usual customer comes, and send the email when twice that period has passed.

## 4. A reminder for a booked appointment

This is the simplest and the most useful. The day before the agreed time you send a short reminder with the time, the address and a phone number for cancelling. It reduces missed appointments, and customers take it as care and not as advertising.

## How to make them pleasant and not annoying

Address the person by name. Give a concrete reason to write right now. Ask for one action only, such as call, book or reply to the email. Make unsubscribing possible with one click. And write only to people who agreed to receive messages from you.

A reminder for a booked appointment is an exception, because it relates to the service the person asked for and does not need separate consent for marketing emails. Seasonal emails and "we have not seen you for a while" emails are marketing, however, and they do require consent.

## What not to do

Do not send the same reminder every week until the person comes. One reminder and one follow up after a week or two are enough. Do not remind someone about a service they have just used, which is why it matters to record when they last came. And do not turn every reminder into an offer. Sometimes plain useful information is stronger than a discount.

## What you need to start

You need a list of customers with their email addresses, the date of the last visit, the service and their consent. If you keep it in a notebook or in Excel, that is enough to begin with. A simple table with a name, an email, the date of the last visit, the service and the date of consent is all an email program needs to send reminders automatically when the time comes.

## How to tell whether it works

Count how many customers booked after the reminder. The easiest way is to ask "Did you get our reminder?" when they book, or to watch the replies and phone calls in the days after the email. If out of a hundred emails five customers come back who might otherwise not have come, that is an excellent result for one automatic message.

## Common questions

**Email or text message?** A text message is read faster and suits short appointment reminders. Email suits longer messages with advice and seasonal campaigns. Many businesses use both.

**How many reminders are too many?** If a customer receives more than one or two emails a month from you and most are not about them, it is too many. Watch the unsubscribes, and if they rise, write less often.
