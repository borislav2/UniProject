---
Until recently, when someone looked for a service, they opened Google and browsed the results. Today more and more people ask an AI assistant directly, whether that is ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or the AI answers at the top of Google itself. The assistant does not show ten links. It replies in a few sentences and sometimes names specific businesses.

**GEO**, which stands for Generative Engine Optimization, is the work of making sure assistants have clear and correct information about your business to answer from.

## What it looks like in practice

Imagine someone types into ChatGPT "I am looking for a children's dentist in Varna who works on Saturdays." The assistant searches the internet, reads several websites and the Google profiles of the practices, and replies with two or three practices and a short reason for each.

If nowhere on your website does it say that you treat children and that you are open on Saturday, the assistant cannot know it. You may be the best practice in town and still miss the answer.

## Where AI assistants learn about you

They use the same places people do. Your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, directories and articles that mention you. If the information is scattered, contradictory or missing, the assistant has nothing to answer from.

There are two main ways they reach information. The first is what they have "learned". The models are trained on a huge amount of text from the internet up to a certain date, so if people have written about you for years, they may know something. The second is a search made at that moment. When a person asks about something current or local, the assistant searches the internet and reads the pages. Here it matters how your site looks today and whether the assistant's bot can access it at all.

## GEO and SEO, and the difference

SEO is the work of ranking higher in the list of Google results. GEO is the work of being cited in the assistant's answer. In practice they overlap a lot, because a clear, fast, well structured site with correct information helps both.

The difference is in the emphasis. With GEO it matters even more that the information is written as an answer to a question and not as advertising copy. It matters that it is the same everywhere, so that the assistant can trust it. And it matters that it contains concrete facts such as prices, deadlines, areas served and opening hours.

## What helps

Clear answers on the website help most. A page of frequently asked questions where it says directly "Yes, we work on Saturdays", "A check-up costs this much" or "We serve Varna and Aksakovo" is very useful. The same name, address, phone number and opening hours on your site, in Google and in directories make you easy to trust.

Structured data helps too. This is special code in the site, known as schema.org, which tells machines what you are, for example a dental practice at this address with these opening hours. Reviews with details are also valuable, because "They replaced my clutch in one day" says much more than five stars without text.

Access for the bots matters as well. If the robots.txt file of your site blocks the search bots of ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude, they cannot read your pages. You can safely block training bots such as GPTBot, but not the ones that search in order to cite your site. Finally, mentions elsewhere help, such as local sites, directories, articles and partners. The more trustworthy places say the same thing about you, the more confidently the assistant will repeat it.

## How to check what they know about you

Open ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity and ask the question the way your customer would. For example, ask who is a good car service in Burgas for suspension repair, or ask what the assistant knows about your business in your city.

See whether you are mentioned, whether the information is correct and where it comes from. If the assistant shows sources, click them. You will often find an old directory with a wrong phone number or a profile with outdated hours.

## What we cannot promise

Nobody can promise that ChatGPT will recommend you, and you should not believe anyone who does. What we can do is make the information about you available, correct and easy to understand. The rest is decided by the assistant.

## Common questions

**Will Google stop being important?** Not soon. Most people still search in Google, and Google itself shows AI answers at the top. Work on visibility in Google and visibility in AI assistants goes hand in hand.

**Do I need a special file for AI?** Some sites add a file called llms.txt with a short description of the business for AI assistants. We have one. It is useful but not mandatory, and it does not replace clear content on the site itself.

**How do I know if customers come from AI assistants?** In Google Analytics, visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com show up as separate sources. For our clients we report enquiries from them as a separate channel.
