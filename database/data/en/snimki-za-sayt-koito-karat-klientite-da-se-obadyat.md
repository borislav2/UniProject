Website Photos That Make Customers Pick Up the Phone
website-photos-that-make-customers-call
Your own photos or stock ones, what to photograph and how heavy the files should be. Practical advice for a small local business website.
---
A person looking for a dentist, a car service or a room for the weekend usually looks at the photos first and reads only afterwards. In a few seconds they decide whether the place looks real, clean and trustworthy. That is why the photos on your site work like a shop window, and a shop window either invites people in or makes them walk past.

## Your own photos or stock ones

Stock photos are convenient because they are ready, beautiful and often cheap. The problem is that people recognise them. A smiling team around a laptop, a handshake over a table or a girl in a headset at a reception desk appear on thousands of sites, and the visitor feels they are looking at something that has nothing to do with you. When they are looking for a specific person to fix their car or cut their child's hair, they want to see exactly that person and exactly that place.

Your own photos do not have to be perfect to be better than stock ones. A car service in Burgas, for example, wins more trust with a real photo of the workshop and the mechanic next to the lift than with a glossy picture of somebody else's garage. Small imperfections are proof that the place exists.

That does not mean stock photos are forbidden. They work for general illustrations, for example in an article of tips or as a background for a section where you cannot show anything specific. Just do not put them where the visitor expects to see you, your team and your product.

## What to photograph

Start with the people, because the customer feels the most interest in them. One good photo of the owner or the team, taken at the workplace in natural light, says more than a whole paragraph about your experience. If someone is hesitating about calling you, a face next to a name helps them overcome that fear.

Then show the place. The entrance, the reception, the work area and the parking are things people imagine in advance. A guest at a house in Bansko wants to know how the room looks, but also the yard and where to leave the car. If the customer sees the entrance beforehand, they will feel calmer when they get there.

Show the work itself too. A hairdresser can photograph the process and the final look, a butcher his display, and an accountant the office with its tidy folders. The aim is not to boast but to make your work visible. When the service is invisible, as with accounting, show the setting and the people and not abstract symbols.

Finally think about photos that answer questions. How to get to you, where the entrance is, what the sign looks like. A photo like that saves unnecessary calls and brings you customers who arrive better prepared. It also helps people who worry about getting lost on the way to you.

## How to photograph without being a photographer

You do not need expensive equipment. A modern phone takes perfectly decent photos if you follow a few simple rules. Shoot in the daytime by a window or outside, because natural light is better than a ceiling lamp. Clear the frame of unnecessary items, cables and rubbish before you press the button. Hold the phone horizontally if the photo will sit as a wide strip at the top of the page.

Take more than you need and then choose the best ones. From twenty shots, two or three are usually good enough for the site. If the budget allows, an hour with a local photographer for the people and the place is a good investment, because those photos will serve for years, on the site, in the Google profile and in ads.

## File size

Here many sites go wrong without knowing it. A phone photo often weighs three, four or more megabytes, and if you put ten of them on one page, the site loads slowly, especially on mobile internet. A patient visitor is rare, and most simply close the page.

That is why photos are reduced before uploading. For a wide photo at the top of the page a width of about 1600 pixels is usually enough, and for a photo in the text about 1000 is sufficient. The WebP format gives a much smaller file at the same quality, and most modern sites support it. A good goal is for each photo to weigh a few hundred kilobytes and not megabytes.

Do not forget the description of the photo, added when you upload it, the so called alternative text. It helps people with poor eyesight and search engines understand what they see. Write it as an ordinary sentence, for example "The mechanic checks the brakes of a car in our workshop".

## A small check before you publish

Look at the page on a phone, because that is where most visitors come from. Try it with Wi-Fi switched off as well, to see how the site looks on a weaker connection, which people on the move often have.
