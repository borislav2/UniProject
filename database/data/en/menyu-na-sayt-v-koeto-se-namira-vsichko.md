Website Menu Design So Visitors Find Everything
website-menu-design-that-helps-visitors
How to arrange your website menu so visitors find the phone number, services and prices quickly, without wandering between pages.
---
Imagine walking into a shop and finding neither a sign nor an assistant. You wander between the shelves, look left and right and after a minute you leave. That is exactly how visitors to a site with a confusing menu feel. They do not get angry, they just quietly leave and go to the next result in Google.

## The menu is a map, not decoration

A menu has one job, which is to show a person where to go. They arrive with a specific aim, such as seeing a price, finding an address, booking a time or checking whether you do exactly what they need. If they cannot reach the answer in a few seconds, their patience runs out. The less they have to think while they search, the more likely they are to stay and contact you.

Many firms put in the menu what seems important to them instead of what the customer is looking for. That is how sections such as Our Philosophy or See More appear, which tell the visitor nothing. An accounting firm in Varna would help its customers far more with sections such as Services, Prices and Contacts.

## Few sections and clear names

The good news is that less is better. For most small firms five to seven sections are quite enough. When you put too many things in one bar, the eye gets lost and nothing stands out.

Name the sections with the words people use. If you are a dental practice, Treatments is clearer than Our Solutions. If you are a guest house in Bansko, Rooms and How to Get Here are more useful than Experiences. Think how the customer would ask on the phone, and use the same words.

Avoid two sections that sound the same, such as Services and What We Offer. The visitor will wonder which of the two to open and will waste time.

Arrange the sections by importance, with the most important also the most visible. The most sought things are usually the services, the prices or at least a guide to them, and a way to contact you. They should be where the eye expects to see them, which is at the top of the page, most often on the left or in the middle.

The phone number and the enquiry button deserve a special place, whatever the menu looks like. A person who has decided to call should not have to hunt for the number at the bottom of the page. On a mobile phone the number must be tappable and start a call at once.

The enquiry button is often styled differently from the other sections, for example as a filled button at the end of the bar. That way it stands apart from the reading sections and shows that it is an action.

## The mobile menu

Most visitors today come from a phone, where the menu collapses into a small icon. It is convenient but also risky, because people do not always realise that the whole content hides behind it. So check how your site looks on a small screen and whether the icon is noticeable.

When you open the menu on a phone, the sections must be big enough for a finger. Small, narrow links lead to accidental taps and irritation. If the menu has subsections, do not bury them in three levels, because on a phone that is tiring.

If you have many services, a submenu can help. Submenus are useful when you really do have many services. A car service with engine repair, suspension, electrical systems and tyres can group them under Services. But the submenu should not be a dumping ground for everything you did not know where to put.

If your site has many pages, such as an online shop, add a search box too. It rescues the visitor who knows what they want but not where to find it. For a site with about ten pages, however, search is usually unnecessary, because a well arranged menu does the same job. The most important thing is that people do not guess where something is, but find it where they expect it.

## How to test it

The simplest test is to ask someone who does not know your business to complete one task. For example, to find the price of a certain service or to work out how to contact you. Watch silently without hinting. Wherever they stumble, the menu has a problem.

You can also go through the site yourself like a stranger, on a phone, with a fresh page open. Ask yourself whether it is clear on every page where you are and how to get back.
