Local SEO for a Business With Several Cities or Locations
local-seo-for-multiple-locations
Do you have locations in several cities? Each needs its own Google profile and its own page on the site. Here is how to arrange your local visibility.
---
A beauty salon in Plovdiv opens a second location in Sofia. The owner is sure she already knows Google, because the first salon shows well on the map. But after a few weeks she finds that people in Sofia see nothing of her, and in Plovdiv the search shows an old address. That is how many of the problems of businesses with more than one city begin. Local search works at the level of the place and not of the company, and each location has to be looked after separately.

## Google looks at every place separately

When someone near you searches for a salon, a dentist or a car service, Google shows a map with a few suggestions. They come from the Google Maps profiles that businesses have created for their locations. One profile is tied to one address. If you have three locations, you need three profiles and not one shared profile that lists the cities.

This is often missed, because the site is one and it seems logical to have one profile. In reality, a person in Burgas looking for the nearest address gets a result according to the distance to a specific place. A profile without an exact address in their city simply cannot be offered.

## The site must have a page for every location

The second half of the task is the site. It is good to have a separate page for each location with the address, phone, opening hours, how to get there and a few real photos of the place. That way Google and the visitor understand that the location exists and is not just a line in a list of cities.

Here a common mistake is easy to make. The firm copies the same text for all the cities and changes only the name of the city. Such pages look as if they were poured from one mould and rarely help. It is far better to write something real for each location, for example about the neighbourhood, the parking nearby or the specialist who works exactly there. If Maria works in Plovdiv and another person in Sofia, mention that.

## The same details everywhere

The name, address and phone of each location must be written in the same way in the Google profile, on the site, on Facebook and in the directories where the firm is present. Even small differences, such as an abbreviation of the street or a different phone number, create uncertainty, and search engines prefer information that repeats consistently.

It is good for each location to have its own phone number, or at least a clearly stated extension, so that you know where calls come from. If the whole team answers one number, it will be hard to tell which location really does better.

## Reviews are separate for each location

Reviews are collected at the level of the profile. If your first location has many good reviews, the second starts from zero and that is quite normal. Do not try to transfer the reviews or write them yourself, because Google has rules against this and the risk is that your profile is affected.

A more sensible path is to ask happy customers at the new location to leave an opinion about it, and to answer every review, good or bad, calmly and with specifics. It is slow work, but it is what builds trust in a given place. Help with profiles and reviews is on our page about the [Google profile and reviews](/en/services/google-business-profile).

## Do not try to appear where you do not work

It is easy to be tempted to create profiles for cities where you have no real location, just to appear there. Google requires a profile to be for a real place where customers can visit you or from where you provide the service. A fake address can lead to the profile being suspended and can harm your other locations.

If you serve customers in other cities without having an office there, for example a tradesman who travels, there is a way to state your service area. Then the profile does not show an address but the areas you reach. That is the honest option and it works well for services at the customer's place.

## What to start with

Make a sheet for each location with the exact name, address, phone and hours. Then check that each one has its own profile and its own page on the site, and that the details match everywhere. You will notice differences that have been there for years.

It also helps to decide who in the firm is responsible for each profile. When the manager of the Plovdiv location changes, someone must know who has access to the profile and be able to transfer it, and not hunt for old emails months later. These small organisational things decide whether your profiles stay up to date.
