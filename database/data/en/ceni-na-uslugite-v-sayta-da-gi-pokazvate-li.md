Should You Show Service Prices on Your Website
should-you-show-prices-on-your-website
Should you show prices on your site? The advantages, the risks and middle solutions that suit the services of a small business.
---
Few questions divide business owners the way this one does. Some say the price must be in plain view so they do not waste time on people who cannot pay it. Others fear that the competition will see it and undercut them, or that the customer will give up before hearing the explanation. Both sides have a point, and the right answer depends on what you sell.

## What you gain by showing the price

The biggest benefit is trust. When a person sees a price, they understand that no unpleasant surprise awaits them, and they take the next step more easily. Many people do not phone a business that hides its price, because they worry about being pressed into buying something expensive.

A visible price also saves time. People for whom the price is too high drop out by themselves and do not occupy you with conversations that would never lead to a sale. A hair salon in Plovdiv with a price list will not answer the same question on the phone every day.

The price also shows what kind of business you are. If you offer a quality service at a reasonable price, openness underlines your confidence. A customer who has seen the price and still calls you is usually ready to talk about a specific job, and the conversation is shorter and calmer.

## What you risk

The risk is real. When a price stands without explanation, the visitor compares it with someone else's price without knowing what it includes. Your service may cost more because it is more complete, but that cannot be seen from one line in a table.

A second problem is that many services do not have a single price. An accountant in Varna cannot name a price before knowing how many documents the company has, whether it is VAT registered and how many employees it has. If you write a number that applies to the simplest case, people will take it as a sure price and be disappointed when they hear something else.

There is also a danger that the price causes a premature refusal. A person who sees a number and leaves never hears about the full scope of the service.

## When showing works best

A price is easiest to show when you offer a standard product or a service with a clear scope. A haircut, a dental check-up, a night in a room or an oil change are things that can be described and priced in advance. Here a hidden price usually only irritates.

When your customers compare offers, and in most fields they do, openness gives you an advantage, because others often stay silent. People value a business that speaks openly.

## The middle solutions

Between the two extremes there are several sensible paths. You can show a "from" price that hints at the start of the scale and explain what the final sum depends on. That way the visitor gets a guide without being misled.

You can also show example price ranges for typical cases, for example "usually between this and that". That honestly expresses that the price varies and still gives an idea.

Another option is packages with a clear description of what they include, with each package having its own price. They help people compare and choose instead of guessing what they get. And finally, if you really cannot name a number, explain how the price is formed and how fast you will answer an enquiry, so that silence does not look like concealment.

## How to decide

Ask yourself which questions customers ask you most often. If half of them start with how much it costs, that is a sign they miss the price. Think also about how many different cases you have, and whether you can describe them in a few lines.

Remember that the decision is not final and that you can change it according to what you see. You can show prices for some of the services and see how people react. If the enquiries become better prepared, continue, and if something gets worse, go back. The aim is for the site to help the customer decide and not to push them. If they feel you are speaking openly, even a high price does not put them off.

Whatever you choose, be consistent. If you show prices, update them regularly, because an outdated price on the site is worse than no price. The price on the site, in ads and in the phone conversation must be the same, because a mismatch destroys trust quickly.
