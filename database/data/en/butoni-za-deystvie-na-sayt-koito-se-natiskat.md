Call to Action Buttons on a Website That People Click
call-to-action-buttons-that-get-clicked
How to make the buttons on your site clear and visible, with the text, colour, place and size that help people call or buy.
---
You can have a beautiful site, many visitors and good texts, and the phone still stays silent. Often the reason is simple. People do not know what to do next. The call to action button is where a reader turns from curious into a customer, and it deserves more attention than we usually give it.

## One button, one thought

Every page has one main aim that you should be able to say in a sentence. On a services page it may be an enquiry, on a product page an order, and on the contact page a phone call. When you put four different buttons of equal importance, the visitor does not know which to choose and most often presses none.

Choose the main action for the page and make it obvious. The other options can remain, but more quietly, as ordinary text with a link. A dental practice in Burgas, for example, would want a clear Book an appointment on every page, and the other links should not compete with it.

## The text on the button

The words on the button are the most important words on the page. General labels such as Send or Learn more do not say what will happen. Verbs that describe the result work better, such as Book an appointment, Request a quote or Call now.

Write from the customer's point of view and not from yours. Ask yourself what they would say if they had to ask for the same thing out loud in a shop. "I want a quote" sounds more natural than "Our offers". When someone reads the label, they should understand what they get and what will happen after they press.

If the action is linked to some worry, calm it in the next line. For example, "We reply within the working day" under the enquiry button removes the fear that nobody will answer. But do not promise something you cannot deliver.

## Colour, size and place

The button must stand out from the rest of the page. If the whole site is in shades of blue, a yellow or orange button is noticed at once. If the button is the same colour as the background, nobody will see it, however good the text.

Size matters too, above all on a phone, where a finger is far less precise than a mouse. A button that is too small is hard to press with a finger and leads to mistakes. Leave enough space around it so it is not confused with the neighbouring elements.

Place is the other half of the task, so think about it separately. The main button goes at the very start of the page, so that no scrolling is needed, and it is repeated lower down, where the reader is already convinced. On a long page one button at the end is not enough, because a person may decide in the middle.

## Buttons on a phone

Most visitors browse a site with one hand. A call button that is tappable and dials the number at once is extremely useful for a local business. A car service with such a button easily gets calls from people standing next to their broken down car.

Some sites also add a bar of buttons at the bottom of the screen that stays visible while scrolling. It can help, but it must not cover the content or annoy people. Try it yourself on a phone and judge whether it gets in your way.

## How to tell if it works

Do not rely only on a feeling. Look at how many enquiries you receive, where they come from and on which page they happen. If a page has many visitors and nobody makes contact, there is probably a problem with the button or with the promise around it. The free Google Search Console and a simple count of your calls help you see where people come from.

Try one change at a time. Change the text of the button and wait a few weeks before touching the colour. That way you will know what worked, and not just that something worked.

A button cannot replace a clear promise, good photos and honest answers to the customer's questions. It is only the end of the road. If the text above it does not convince, a beautiful button will not help. People press when they have already understood what you offer, at what price and why they should trust you.

## A small check today

Open your site on a phone and ask an acquaintance to tell you what they would have to do to contact you. If they hesitate, the button is not clear enough.
