When Domain and Hosting Expire and How Not to Lose Your Site

An expired domain means a website that will not open and emails that stop working. Here is how to track the dates and lose nothing.
---
One morning the owner of a small hotel in Bansko finds that the website does not open, and the business emails neither arrive nor leave. No bookings come in, and customers who wrote get no reply. After an hour of calls it turns out that the domain expired the day before. Nobody received a warning, or so it seems, because the messages were arriving at the mailbox of the person who built the site three years ago and no longer works there. A story like this sounds like an exception, but an unrenewed domain is among the most annoying reasons for a site to vanish suddenly.

## The domain and the hosting are two different things

The domain is the address of your site, for example yourcompany.bg. The hosting is the place where the site itself is kept. The two services are paid separately, often in different places, and have different dates. A domain is usually renewed for one or more years. Hosting may be paid monthly or yearly.

This detail confuses many, because when an outside firm built the site, it often registered both and pays for them in its own name. That leaves the key information in its hands, and you do not even know where the services are held.

## What happens when the date passes

When a domain expires, the site stops opening. Along with it stop all the email addresses that use the same domain, because the mail is also run through it. For some firms that is the hardest part, since customers, suppliers and the bank write to exactly that address.

There is usually a short grace period in which the domain can still be renewed, but it depends on the kind of domain and the registrar, and you should not rely on it. After that period the domain can be released and bought by somebody else. Then getting it back may be very hard or impossible.

For hosting the rules are different. Many providers suspend the site for non payment, and after a set period delete the data. How long that period is, you must check in your provider's terms, because it differs everywhere.

## Where the warnings go

The reminder system almost always works, but the messages go to an email that someone gave at registration. If that is the personal mailbox of a former colleague, of a firm you no longer work with, or an old address that expired together with the domain, nobody in your company receives them.

The most important thing you can do is to check in whose name the domain is registered and which email is listed as the contact. The company from which you bought the domain gives you access to an account where you can see the owner, the contact email and the expiry date. If you have no such account, the domain is most likely registered with someone else, and that is the first thing to clear up. The domain must be registered to your company or to you personally, and not to the company that made the site.

## How to get organised

Begin with a sheet where you write three things. Where the domain is registered, until when it is paid and who has access to the account. Then the same for the hosting. Then mark in your calendar a date a month before each deadline and another two weeks before it, so that you do not rely only on warnings by email.

Turn on automatic renewal of the domain when it is possible, and make sure the card used for payment does not expire in the same month. Automatic renewal often fails precisely because the card has expired.

A frequently missed detail is the certificate for the secure connection, the padlock next to the address. It also has an expiry date, although with most hosting providers it renews itself. If it expires, browsers start to warn visitors that the site is not safe, and many of them leave without reading anything.

Keep the login details in a safe place that is accessible to at least two people in the firm. If the only person who knows the password is on holiday or has left, one deadline can cost you more than you expect.
