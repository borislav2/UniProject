Google Reviews for Local Businesses and How to Reply
google-reviews-how-to-get-and-answer
Reviews are among the first things people check before they call you. Here is how to collect them regularly and how to answer a bad one.
---
Before they phone an unfamiliar business, most people look at the stars and reviews in Google. The good news is that happy customers usually do not mind writing one. Nobody has simply asked them.

## Why reviews matter so much

For people, a review is the opinion of someone like them and not your advertising. That is especially true for services you cannot try first, such as a dentist, a repair or a lawyer. For Google, the number of reviews, the rating and how recently the last one was written all influence how high you appear on the map. For AI assistants, when they recommend a business they often summarise what people write about it, and reviews with details give them something to summarise.

## How to get reviews

Ask at the right moment, which is right after the service, when the customer is happy and still with you. Make it easy. In your Google Business Profile press the button to ask for reviews, copy the link and send it by text message or email. One tap and the person is in the form. You can add it to the email after the service, for example "Thank you! If you are happy, we would appreciate two sentences in Google, and here is the link." A QR code at the till or on your business card that leads to the form also works.

A review that just says "Great!" with five stars helps, but a review with details helps much more. You can guide the customer without dictating. For example, "If you have a minute, we would love you to write in Google how the repair went and how long it took. It helps other people decide." That way the person remembers to mention the service, the time and the attitude.

A sample text message might be "Hello Ivan! Thank you for choosing Auto Sever. If you are happy with the repair, we would appreciate a short review in Google, and here is the link. Best wishes, Peter." It is short and personal, with a name and a direct link.

## What you must not do

Do not buy reviews or write them yourself. Google detects them and can delete them, and people can sense them. Do not offer a discount in return for a positive review, because that breaks Google's rules. Do not ask only the happy customers and conveniently forget the unhappy ones, because Google calls that selective review gating and it is also against the rules. Ask everyone, and answer the bad reviews well. And do not ask relatives and friends to write. It looks harmless, but it shows, especially when all the reviews arrive in one week.

## How to answer a good review

Keep it short and personal. "Thank you, Maria! We are glad the car was ready on time. Come back for the check in spring." The reply shows future readers that there are people behind the business.

Do not copy the same reply under every review. "Thank you for your review" twenty times in a row looks automatic. Mention something specific from the review.

## How to answer a bad review

Wait until you have calmed down, and never reply angry. Thank the person and acknowledge the problem without arguing, for example "We are sorry you had to wait." Explain what you did or offer a solution. Then move the conversation out of Google by inviting them to call you so you can put it right.

People who read reviews look not only at the rating but also at how you react. A calm reply to a bad review often wins more trust than ten glowing ones.

Here is an example. A two star review says "I waited 40 minutes even though I had an appointment. Nobody told me anything." A good reply is "Hello Elena, we are sorry about the wait and that nobody warned you. We had an emergency that day, but we should have told you at once. We now warn patients by text message when there is a delay. If you like, call us and we will book you a convenient time without waiting. Dr Petrova." It acknowledges, explains without excuses, names a concrete change and makes an offer, with no argument.

## What to do with a fake or abusive review

If the review comes from someone who was never your customer, or contains insults, personal data or comes from a competitor, you can report it to Google from your profile. Google does not remove reviews just because they are negative, but it does remove ones that break its rules. While you wait, write a short, calm reply such as "We could not find a visit under this name in our schedule. If you were with us, please call so we can clarify." That helps readers understand the situation.

## How many reviews you need

There is no magic number. It is more important that they arrive **regularly**. A profile with 30 reviews and the last one from yesterday looks more alive than one with 100 reviews and the last one two years ago. The goal is new reviews every month.

## Common questions

**Can I delete a bad review?** No, only Google can remove it, and only if it breaks the rules. That is why a calm and helpful reply is the best answer.

**Do I have to answer every review?** Yes, if you can. If there are many, prioritise the bad ones and those with text. The reply is for the next readers, not only for the person who wrote.

**How fast should I reply?** Within a day or two is good. For a bad review it is important to reply after you have checked what happened, but not after weeks.
