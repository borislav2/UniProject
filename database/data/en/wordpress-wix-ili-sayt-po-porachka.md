WordPress, Wix or a Custom Website for a Small Business
wordpress-wix-or-custom-website
An honest comparison of WordPress, Wix and a custom site. Which suits a small business, and what to ask before you choose.
---
When the owner of a small company decides they need a website, they soon hear three different pieces of advice from three different people. One acquaintance says only WordPress, another insists that Wix is the easiest, and a third proposes something built specially for them. All three are sincere, all three are partly right, and that is why the choice looks hard.

## There is no best platform

The truth is that none of the three options is bad in itself. Each is good for a certain kind of business and awkward for another. So the question is not which one is best, but which one suits your situation, the time you have and what you want to do after the site goes live.

Before you compare, answer three things. How often will you change the content? Do you have someone to look after the site? And do you need something unusual, such as bookings, orders or a connection to a program you use?

## Wix and similar builders

Builders are made for people who want a site quickly and do not want to think about technology. Hosting, security and updates are included, and pages are arranged by dragging. A hair salon in Plovdiv that wants a page with services, photos and a phone number can start within a few days.

The price of this ease is the limit. You work within what the builder allows, and with an unusual idea you quickly hit a wall. The site lives with the company that provides it, and moving it elsewhere usually means building it again. The monthly fee also continues as long as the site is alive.

That is not a drawback if your needs are simple and stable. The problem comes when the business grows and the site stays where it was. For many very small firms that just want to be found and to look decent, a builder is perfectly enough, and there is nothing to be ashamed of.

## WordPress

WordPress is the most widespread website system in the world, and that is a real advantage. There is a huge choice of ready themes and plugins, many specialists know how to work with it, and the site is yours and can be moved to another hosting company. For a business that wants a blog, service pages and room for future growth, it is a reliable base.

Against that flexibility stands the care it needs. WordPress and its plugins must be updated regularly, or the site becomes vulnerable. Backups, security and speed do not happen by themselves either. If nobody takes this on, the site starts to fall apart quietly, and the owner usually finds out late.

Many bad impressions of WordPress are really impressions of neglected sites. Well maintained, it is a thoroughly worthy choice. Ask anyone who uses it who updates it, and you will learn a lot about how the site will look in a year.

## A custom website

A site built specially for you has no unnecessary parts and is made exactly around your process. It is usually faster because it does not carry the weight of dozens of plugins, and it allows what templates cannot, such as specific booking logic or an unusual look. For a business with a clear idea this is a big advantage.

The drawback is dependence. If the site is built so that only one person understands it, and that person disappears, you are in a difficult position. So with this option ask in advance who owns the code, how changes will be made and what happens if you part ways with the contractor. A good contractor will answer calmly and put it in writing. Ask for access to the domain and hosting in your own name too, because that is the simplest protection against unpleasant surprises.

## How to choose

If you have little time, a simple business and do not expect big changes, a builder is a sensible start. If you want a blog, constant development and control over the site, WordPress with someone who maintains it is a strong choice. If you have specific needs or want maximum speed and a clean look, a custom site deserves serious attention.

Think about the distant future as well. Ask yourself whether in two years you will want to change platform and how much that would cost. Moving is possible everywhere, but in some places it is much more expensive and harder.

Always ask what the maintenance will cost, not only the build. A cheap start sometimes hides regular costs, and an expensive start sometimes has none.
