Why Google Does Not Show My Website and How to Check
why-google-does-not-show-my-website
Your site is missing from Google? Learn how to check whether it is indexed, the most common reasons and what you can do today.
---
Imagine a car service in Burgas whose website has been ready for two months. The owner types the name of the service into Google and sees the Facebook page, one directory and nothing more. Behind this complaint stand two different questions that are easily mixed up. The first is whether Google even knows the page exists. The second is whether it considers the page useful enough to show in a good position. The two problems are cured in different ways, so first you must find out which one you have.

## What it means for a site to be indexed

Google keeps a huge database of pages it has already visited and read. When someone searches for something, the search engine chooses an answer from that database and not from the live internet at that moment. If your page has not got into it, nobody will find it through search, however well it is written. Getting into the database is called indexing.

Indexing is not a reward for a good site. It is only the first step, after which comes the question of where the page will be shown. Many people think that if the site opens in their browser, Google sees it automatically. That is not so, especially with a new address that almost nobody links to.

## The quickest check you can do yourself

Open Google and type the word site, then straight after it a colon, and after the colon the address of your site, with no spaces and without http in front. The search engine will show the pages it knows from that domain. If you see several results, then at least part of the site is indexed. If nothing appears, that is a serious sign that something is in the way.

This method is rough and sometimes gives an incomplete picture, so take it as a first orientation. A more accurate tool is Google Search Console, which is free. You connect it to your site after confirming that the site is yours, and you get a field for inspecting a specific address. You paste the address of the page and get a clear answer whether it is in the Google database, when it was last visited and, if it is not, for what reason.

## The most common reasons a page is not shown

The most unpleasant reason is also the easiest to fix. While it is being built, a site is often kept closed to search engines so that half finished pages are not visible. This is done with a setting that tells Google not to index anything. Sometimes the setting stays on after the site goes live, and nobody remembers to remove it. It happens most when several different people took part in the work.

The second reason is the robots.txt file, which may forbid search engines from accessing the site. It is a small text file, and one wrong line in it is enough to close the whole place. The third reason is the lack of a sitemap, which helps Google find all the pages. The fourth is very practical. If the site is new and nobody points to it, the search engine may simply not have got there yet. That usually takes from a few days to a few weeks, sometimes more, and nobody can give you an exact time.

There are quieter reasons too. A page with very little text, with content copied from another site, or with the same title as the others is often indexed slowly or shown badly. Google is not obliged to put every page offered to it into its database.

## What to do if the site is not indexed

First confirm your site in Search Console, because without that you will be guessing. Then inspect the address of the home page with the address inspection tool and read what it says about the reason. If the page is blocked, look in the system the site was made with for the visibility setting. In many systems there is a box for hiding the site from search engines, and it is easy to overlook.

Then submit the sitemap through Search Console, if you have one, and make sure robots.txt does not contain a ban on the whole site. It is also good for other places to point to you, for example your Google profile, your Facebook page and the company directories where you are already listed. These are natural entrances through which a search engine finds new addresses.

If you have been through all this and the pages are still missing, the cause is probably not technical. Then look at the content and ask yourself whether each page answers something people really search for. ## What not to expect

An indexed site does not mean it will appear first. It only enters the race. The position depends on the competition in your city, on the content and on many other things for which nobody can give a guarantee. If someone has promised you first place within a set time, do not take the promise seriously.
