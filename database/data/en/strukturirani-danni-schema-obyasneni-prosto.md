Structured Data and Schema Explained in Plain Words
structured-data-schema-explained-simply
Structured data is a kind of note for search engines. Here is what it is, why everyone who builds sites mentions it and what you should ask for.
---
If you have ever talked to someone who builds websites, you have probably heard the words schema or structured data. They are often said quickly, in the expectation that everything is clear. In fact the idea is quite simple and does not require you to be technical.

The aim of this text is to explain it so that next time you know what to ask and what to expect.

## What structured data is

When a person opens your site, they see a tidy page and at once understand where the address is, where the opening hours are and where the phone number is. A search engine, however, sees only text. It is not certain whether the number next to the word price is the price of a service or the number of some street.

Structured data is made of invisible notes in the code of the page that tell the search engine clearly what is what. For example, that this is the name of the business, this is the address, this is the opening time, and this is a question with its answer. The visitor does not see them, but Google and other systems can read them.

## Why it matters

When a search engine understands better what a page is about, it can show it more suitably. Sometimes that means extra information under the result, such as a rating, questions and answers or opening hours. In other cases it simply helps the system not to be wrong about what the page is.

You have to be realistic. Structured data does not put you in first place and does not guarantee a special look for the result. Google decides what to show and its rules change. But it is part of work done well, it costs little effort, and when it is missing the site leaves an incomplete impression.

## Which data makes sense for a small business

For most local businesses a few things are the most useful. The first is the data about the business itself, with name, address, phone, opening hours and links to your official profiles. The second is data about services, if you have clearly described services. The third is questions and answers, if you have a page where you answer frequently asked questions.

For a dentist in Plovdiv this would mean describing that it is a dental practice, where it is, when it works and what care it offers. For an online shop there is a separate kind of data for products, such as price and availability.

## What it looks like in practice

Imagine a postcard. On the front there is a beautiful picture, and on the back the address and name are written in clearly marked places. Structured data is the back of the postcard for search engines. The person looks at the picture, and the machine reads the back.

## The most common mistake

The biggest problem is not missing data but wrong data. If it states one opening time and the page another, or if it lists a phone number that is no longer yours, that is worse than having none. Search engines may stop trusting it and customers may be confused.

The second mistake is adding data about things that do not exist. For example reviews that are not real, or a rating that cannot be seen on the page. Google explicitly dislikes this and may restrict how your site is shown. The data must describe only what a person can really see.

## Should you do it yourself

No. This is a job for the person who maintains your site, and often it happens automatically when the site is built well. Many modern site systems do it from the start, but the settings are sometimes incomplete or outdated.

You can check how things stand without understanding the code. Google offers free testing tools in which you paste the address of a page and it shows which data was found and whether there are errors. If you see many warnings, show them to your contractor and ask what they mean.

Ask explicitly whether the site will have structured data for the business and the services, and who will maintain it when the phone number or the opening hours change. It is good to have this written into the quote, so that there is no misunderstanding later. If the contractor answers vaguely, that is a good reason to ask for more detail before you sign.
