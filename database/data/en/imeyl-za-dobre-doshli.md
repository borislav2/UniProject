Welcome Email for New Subscribers With Ready Examples
welcome-email
The welcome email is opened more often than any other you send. Here is what to put in it, with examples for a salon, a car service and a restaurant.
---
When someone subscribes, they have just shown interest in you. That is the moment they are most likely to open your message, which is why the first automatic email is the most important one in your whole email communication. You set it up once, and after that it works on its own for every new subscriber without you having to think about it.

## What it should contain

Begin with thanks and confirmation. Something like "Thank you for signing up!" makes the person sure everything went through. Then say what they will receive and how often, for example "Once a month we will write to you about seasonal offers and useful skin care advice." Give them something useful straight away, such as a tip, a short guide or helpful information, and not only advertising. Add one clear next action, such as see the free appointment times or look at the menu. Only one. Finally, say who is writing. A signature with a real first name sounds different from "The team".

## The subject line

The subject decides whether the email gets opened. For a welcome email it should be clear and not clever. Good examples are "Welcome to Lavandula Studio", "Your 10 percent code is inside" when you promised a discount, or "Thank you, Maria! Here is what comes next". Avoid capital letters, many exclamation marks and words like FREE, which often send the message to the promotions tab or to spam.

## An example for a beauty salon

The subject line reads Welcome to Lavandula Studio. The message follows. Hello Maria, thank you for signing up! Once a month we will write to you about what suits your skin in the current season and when we have free appointments. Here is the first tip. After the summer the skin is often dry. Before you choose a treatment, call us and we will tell you what you need, without selling you anything extra. The free appointment times for next week are on our website. Best wishes, Elena from Lavandula Studio.

## An example for a car service

The subject line reads Thank you, Ivan! We will remind you in time. The message follows. Hello Ivan, we have signed you up for reminders. We will write when it is time for an oil change, for tyres in autumn and spring, and for the annual inspection, and no more than one email a month. Something useful for now. If you hear squeaking when braking, do not wait for the next check, because it is usually a sign that the brake pads are nearly worn, and replacing them in time is much cheaper than replacing the discs. To book a time call us or use the link on our site. Best wishes, Peter, Auto Sever.

## An example for a restaurant

The subject line reads Welcome! Here is this week's menu. The message follows. Hello, thank you for signing up. We will write when we change the menu and when we hold an event, such as a tasting, a festive evening or live music. This week the chef recommends lamb with roasted vegetables and homemade lemonade. To reserve a table call us or use the form on our site. See you soon, The Vine Restaurant.

## One email or a series

For most small businesses one good email is enough at the beginning. If you sell online or have more to tell, it can grow into a short series of two or three emails in the first week. On day zero you send the welcome and the promised gift or discount. On day two you explain who you are, how you work and what makes you different. On day five you share customer reviews and a reminder if the discount is running out.

## Common mistakes

The email may not arrive at all, so check that it does not go to spam, especially with Gmail. It may be too long, and five paragraphs are the maximum. It may be only discounts, because if the first email is just a percentage off, you teach people to wait for a sale. It may be sent from a noreply address, but if the person replies, the answer should reach you, because replies are a good sign and help your emails stay out of spam. And it must always have an unsubscribe link, which the law requires even in the first email.

## How to tell if it works

Email programs show how many people opened the message and how many clicked. Two things matter. The first is clicks, meaning how many people pressed your next action. If they are few, make the button clearer or the offer more concrete. The second is unsubscribes right after the first email. If there are many, the email probably does not match what you promised at sign up.

## Common questions

**Does it need pictures?** Not necessarily. A plain text email that looks like a personal message often works better than a heavy template full of pictures. If you add a picture, make it one, and make it yours.

**How fast should it go out?** Immediately. People expect a confirmation within minutes. If it comes the next day, many have already forgotten they signed up.
