Mobile Friendly Website Test You Can Do in Five Minutes

A large share of people will see your site only on their phone. Here is what you can check yourself, with no technical knowledge.
---
When someone looks for a restaurant for tonight, a car service after the car has stopped, or a locksmith in front of a locked door, they search from their phone. For many local businesses the phone is the main device on which customers see them. Google also judges sites first by their mobile version.

## What mobile first means

Google looks at and evaluates your site the way it appears on a phone. If your site is full of information on a computer but part of it is hidden or cut off on a phone, Google sees the poorer version. If the site is awkward on a phone, that affects how high you appear. In other words, the mobile version is no longer an add on. It is the main one.

## A five minute check

Take your phone and open your site as a customer would. These are the things to look at.

First, can you read it without zooming? The text should be big enough that you do not need to spread two fingers. Second, are the buttons easy to press? If you press one and the neighbour opens, the buttons are too small or too close together. Third, does the page scroll sideways? It should not move left and right. Fourth, can you tap the phone number? The number must be a link that dials directly. Fifth, can you fill in the form? The keyboard should not cover the fields, and digits should appear for the phone number. Sixth, how fast does it load on mobile data? Switch off Wi-Fi and try.

Five more things are often missed. Does the menu open easily and close again, and does it contain everything important or are half the pages missing? Do pop up windows, such as the cookie banner or a subscription window, cover the whole screen without a clear close button? Are photos cropped badly, and can you see faces and products or are they cut in half? Do wide price tables run off the screen? On a phone it is better for them to become a list. And does tapping the address open navigation on the phone?

## What people do on a phone

Behaviour on a phone differs from a computer. People are in a hurry. They are often outside, on the road or between two tasks, and they look for a phone number, an address, opening hours and a price. They scroll and do not read, so the main information must be on the first screens. And they act at once by calling, asking for directions or writing a message, so the buttons for that must be easy to find.

That is why on a phone the most important things must be at the top, which means what you do, the phone number and an enquiry button. The details can be lower down.

## If something is wrong

If the site was made years ago, it was probably not designed for phones. Sometimes it can be fixed with changes to the theme. Sometimes it is cheaper to build it again than to patch it.

How do you judge? With small problems, such as one small button or one cropped photo, the fix is usually quick. If the site simply shrinks like a small copy of the desktop version and you have to zoom, it is probably not responsive at all, and that is rarely fixed well with patches. If the site is slow on a phone but fast on a computer, the problem is often heavy photos and scripts. ## Common questions

**Do I need a separate mobile app?** For most local businesses, no. A good site on a phone does the same job without people having to install anything. An app makes sense if your customers use the service every week, such as ordering food or booking at a gym.

**How do I check how it looks on different phones?** The easiest way is to ask two or three people with different phones, for example an iPhone and an Android, to open the site and try to send you an enquiry. You will learn much more than from any test.
