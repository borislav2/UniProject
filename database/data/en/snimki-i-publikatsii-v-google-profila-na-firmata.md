Photos and Posts in Your Google Business Profile
photos-and-posts-in-google-business-profile
Your Google Maps profile is the first thing many customers see about you. Here is why the photos and posts in it deserve your attention.
---
When someone looks for a hairdresser, a dentist or a car service near them, Google often shows a map with a few businesses. The person glances at the photos, reads a few reviews and decides whom to call. Often all of this happens without opening a single website.

That makes your Google profile something like a shop window on a busy street. The tidier, more current and more honest it is, the more likely the passer by is to come in.

## Photos are what people trust first

The visitor cannot see you in person, so photos give an idea of what to expect. A profile with no photos, or with one blurry picture from years ago, leaves the impression that the business is not very active, even if it is.

Add photos of the entrance and the site from outside, so people recognise it when they arrive. Show the interior, the workplace and the team. If you offer things that can be seen, such as haircuts, repairs or dishes, add examples of your real work. Photos you take yourself with your phone in good light usually work better than over polished ones.

## What to photograph in different businesses

A guest house in Bansko can show the rooms, the view from the window, the breakfast and the entrance, so that guests can find it in the snow. A car service in Burgas can show the workshop, how clean it is and the team in front of it. A beauty salon in Varna will benefit from photos of the premises and of finished work, if customers agree to be seen.

Ask customers before you photograph them, and never publish people's faces without their consent. If you are not sure, photograph the work and not the person.

Do not put in other people's photos, and do not use stock images that show something you do not offer. A customer who sees a difference between the picture and reality is disappointed, and that often shows up in their review.

## Posts show that you are active

Google lets businesses add short posts to their profile. They can announce news, a change in opening hours, a special offer or simply show something new. That way the profile does not look frozen in time.

Write briefly and concretely, in full sentences. For example, say that you work until noon during the holidays, that you are adding a new service or that you have a new piece of equipment. You do not need to post every day. One post every few weeks, when you have something to share, is quite enough.

## Current details matter most of all

Before you think about photos and posts, check that the basics are right. The opening hours, phone, address and website must be accurate. If a customer arrives and finds you closed although the profile says otherwise, that is the surest way to get a bad review.

Update the profile with every change, including on holidays, when the opening hours are almost always different from usual. It takes minutes and prevents a lot of irritation.

## How to tidy the profile in one afternoon

You do not need a whole day. Open your profile and check the basic details. Then go through the photos and remove those that are outdated or show something that no longer exists. Add a few new ones taken in recent weeks. Finally, write one short post about something current at your place.

Then set yourself a small routine. For example, on the first Monday of the month look at the profile, add one photo and answer new reviews. Small regular steps turn out more useful than a big effort made once and forgotten. If you have an employee or a colleague, give them this as a regular task, so that it does not depend on your mood.

## How it ties in with reviews

Photos and posts work together with reviews. A profile with very good reviews but no photos looks incomplete. A profile with photos but no reviews looks new and unproven. It is advisable to answer reviews, both the positive and the more critical ones, calmly and to the point. That shows that there is a person behind the profile who reads what is being said.

None of these things guarantees you a position on the map. Google uses many signals, and the details are not public. But a full and living profile certainly gives you better chances than an empty one, and at the very least it makes a good impression on anyone who opens it.
