Website Backups and How Often You Should Make Them
website-backups-how-often-to-make-them
A backup is the cheapest protection for your site. Here is what it should contain, where to keep it and how often to renew it.
---
One morning in Bansko, the owner of a guest house opens their website and sees that the booking form has vanished and an error message sits in its place. Yesterday everything worked. The cause may be a failed update, a human mistake or an intrusion by a stranger. Whatever the cause, the first question any sensible person asks is whether there is a copy of the site from two days ago. If there is, the problem is unpleasant but fixable. If there is not, something much longer and more expensive begins.

## What a backup actually is

A backup is a complete saved state of the site at a certain moment. It includes the files, the photos, the texts and the database in which the pages, orders and enquiries live. If you have only a copy of the files without the database, you often cannot restore the site, because the most valuable content is exactly in the database.

A copy makes sense only if it is somewhere else. If it sits on the same server as the site, and the server breaks or is attacked, you lose both at once. So it is sensible to keep copies separately from the hosting on which the site runs.

## Why you need one even if your site is small

Many think backups are a worry of big shops. In practice a small site has the same risks. The hosting company may make its own copy, but it is not always included in every plan, not always fresh enough and not always something you can restore yourself. Before you rely on it, ask exactly what the provider keeps, how far back and how it is restored.

The most common cases of loss are quite ordinary. An update of one plugin can break the whole page. A staff member can delete an important page by mistake. The site can be hacked and have foreign links inserted into it. And if you stop paying for the old hosting, the provider usually deletes the data after a set time. In each of these cases a well made copy returns you to a calm state in minutes or hours instead of days, and saves you the explanations to customers.

## How often a copy should be made

There is no single figure that is right for everybody. It helps to ask how much work you would lose if you returned the site to yesterday's state. A business card site for an accountant in Varna, whose content changes a few times a year, may need a copy once a week. A small online shop where orders come in every day should be copied at least once a day, because an order that is not in the copy is lost for ever.

The scheme is usually simple. Sites that receive orders, bookings or enquiries are copied every day. Sites that rarely change are well protected by a weekly copy. And before any bigger change, such as a new design or an update of the main system, it is worth making an extra copy, however well organised the rest is.

How many old copies you keep matters as well. If a problem is discovered only after two weeks, a copy from yesterday will not help, because it already contains the damage. It is sensible to keep copies from several different dates and not only the latest.

## A copy that nobody has tried to restore is only a hope

The most common mistake is believing that a backup works just because the system says it was made. The only way to be sure is to try a restore at least once, preferably in a test place and not on the live site. Then you learn whether the copy is complete, how long the restore takes and who on the team knows how to do it.

Ask who is responsible as well. In small firms backups are often a task that everyone considers someone else's. One thinks the hosting does it, another that the person who built the site does it, and in practice nobody does. Name a person and not a department, and write down when it was last checked.

## What to do this week

Start with one simple question to your hosting provider or to the person who maintains your site. Is there an automatic copy, at what interval, where is it kept and when was the last restore? If you do not get a clear answer, you most likely have no real protection.
