Email List Segmentation for Small Business Explained Simply
email-list-segmentation-explained
Segmentation means writing to people about what concerns them. Here are the simple ways to divide your list and where to start.
---
If you have a small online cosmetics shop, the same email about a discount on shampoos is not equally interesting to all your customers. A woman who buys hair oil every two months and a man who bought a birthday present for a friend a year ago expect different things. Dividing the list into smaller groups, which is called segmentation, sounds like something for big companies. In practice it is something you can do this week with a spreadsheet and a little common sense.

## What a segment is

A segment is simply a group of people on your list who have something in common. It may be the city, an interest, how they got to know you or whether they have bought. You do not need many groups. For most small firms two or three meaningful groups are quite enough.

The idea is simple. Instead of writing the same thing to everyone, you write to each group about something close to them. That way the email looks like a message to a person and not like a mass newsletter, and it is less often deleted without reading.

## The easiest ways to divide the list

The most natural split is between people who are already your customers and people who have only shown interest. The first know what you offer and deserve emails about a new offer, a reminder or a thank you. The second have not yet decided and need calmer information that shows why it is worth trusting you.

Another common split is by city or district. A dental practice in Plovdiv has no reason to invite people from Burgas for a check-up. The same applies to a hair salon, a car service or a guest house, where location decides everything.

A third split is by interest. If at sign up a person noted that they are interested in, say, a car check and not tyres, write to them about what they chose. People find it easier to read something they said they are interested in.

A fourth, often neglected, is the split by recency. A person who bought from you last week and a person who has done nothing for two years should not get the same emails. To the second it is good to write something different, for example a short question whether they still wish to hear from you.

## Where to get the information

You do not need to ask many questions. Every extra field in a form reduces the number of sign ups. It is enough to ask for the email and at most one more thing, for example a city or an interest. The rest you will learn from people's behaviour, such as what they bought and which links they clicked.

If you already have a list without such data, do not despair. You can start with what you know. Customers with a purchase are one group and the others are a second. Then, in the next email, you can include one question that people answer with one click.

## Care for personal data

Segmentation should not be done without a basis. People's email addresses are personal data and they gave them to you for a certain purpose. If they signed up for a newsletter, do not use them for something completely different. Keep the information you have collected tidy and accessible only to those who really need it.

Every email must have a clear unsubscribe link, whatever the segment. If someone asks not to receive any more emails, stop writing to them in all groups and not only in one.

## How to start without complex systems

A table with two columns is enough. In one is the email and in the other the group. Most programs for sending emails let you add labels to people and then send only to those with a certain label. Start with two groups and check after three emails whether opens and clicks differ between them. If the difference is clear, add a third group. If it is not, the split did not matter and you can try another one.

Do not strive for perfection. A small group to whom you write something relevant gives more than a big list to which you write general things. And remember that nobody can tell you exactly what will result, because every firm and every list is different.
