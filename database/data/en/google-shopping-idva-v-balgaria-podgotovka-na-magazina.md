Google Shopping in Bulgaria and How to Prepare Your Shop
google-shopping-bulgaria-prepare-your-shop
There is no official date yet, but shops with ready data win on launch day. What Google checks, what a feed is and what to fix now.
---
Google Shopping is the place where a buyer sees a photo, a price and the name of the shop right in the search results. In many countries it is the main channel for online shops. It is not yet available in Bulgaria, but it may appear soon.

Bogdan Bogomilov, Digital Marketing Manager at Mebeli Videnov and Vellea Global with more than 13 years in PPC, explained at Digital Community Day 2026 what is known, what is not and how to prepare.

## What we know and what we do not

In March 2026 Google announced Shopping for 15 European markets, Bulgaria among them. Bulgaria appeared in Google's documentation on 14 July and disappeared from the list in August. The expected launch is in the first or second quarter of 2027. Bogomilov is firm that Google has not announced an official date as of today. Free listings and Shopping ads are not yet available for Bulgaria.

So there is no reason to panic. There is something to organise so that you are ready when a launch comes. In his words, the first to win are those who have data ready on launch day.

## Why it is worth it

The data on his slides is mostly from other markets, so read it as a direction and not as a forecast for Bulgaria. According to Tinuiti in the US, 85 percent of clicks on retailer ads in Google Search come from Shopping, and 76 percent of paid search budget goes there. According to State of PPC 2026 in the US, a Shopping click is 22 percent cheaper than a click in ordinary paid search. Of searches in Bulgaria, 96 percent go through Google, according to StatCounter in September 2026.

Online sales in Bulgaria were 2.69 billion euro in 2025, which is 15 percent more, according to the Bulgarian E-commerce Association in its Passport 2025. But only 57 percent of internet users shop online, the lowest share in the EU, according to Eurostat 2025. There is room to grow.

## What Google checks

Google looks at the site as a buyer would. It looks for clear contacts and company details, delivery and return terms, prices and payment methods, and HTTPS. Without clear contacts and policies the account can be suspended. The data in Merchant Center must match the site exactly, including the name, address, postcode, phone, website address, delivery and returns. If the site shows one delivery price and the feed another, you have a problem.

## The feed is the heart of Shopping

The feed is a file with all your products, with the name, description, price, availability and photos. Google reads it and builds the listings from it. Bogomilov sums it up by saying that in Shopping the quality of the data matters more than the creative.

A good feed has a **title** of 70 to 100 characters containing the brand, type, model and material. Not just "Bowl" but the brand, then "Stainless steel dog bowl 1.2 litres". It has a **description** of 700 to 1,000 characters. It has a **price and availability** that match the site, and the feed is refreshed at least once a day. And it has **photos** of at least 500 by 500 pixels from 31 January 2027. Google data quoted by Digitas Sofia shows that five or more photos give 76 percent more impressions and 32 percent more clicks.

From the same data, a promotional price raises CTR by 7.6 percent, ratings by 5 percent, and free delivery adds 2 percent to conversions.

Do not forget the margin either. Bogomilov suggests splitting products by profit and working out the minimum ROAS as 1 divided by the margin. With a 25 percent margin that is a ROAS of 4. With a lower ROAS the ads do not pay back their costs at that margin.

## What if the shop is not on WooCommerce or Shopify

The feed is built from product data, and every shop has that. How exactly we take it, whether through a ready extension, an export or a file, depends on the system. That is why the first step is a short review of the shop and not the choice of a tool. If the product data is scattered across spreadsheets, putting it in order is the larger part of the work.

## What to do now

Start by reviewing contacts, delivery, returns and terms, and make sure everything is visible from every page. Put the product data in order, with full titles, descriptions, prices and stock levels. Replace small photos and add more per product. Add structured data for products to the site. Set up purchase measurement in GA4 so you know what brings money. And create a Merchant Center account and verify your site.

None of this is wasted effort, even if Google postpones the launch. Clear policies and good data help both the buyer and your other channels.
