Monthly Website Checklist for Small Business Owners
monthly-website-checklist
A website is never finished for good. Here is a short checklist that takes 15 minutes once a month and saves you unpleasant surprises.
---
Your website works around the clock, even when you are not looking at it. Sometimes it stops working and nobody tells you. Here is what to check once a month, and it takes about 15 minutes.

Set a reminder in your calendar, for example for the first Monday of the month, and go through the points below one by one.

## 1. The enquiry form

Send yourself an enquiry from the website. Did it reach your inbox, and is it not in spam? This is the most important check, because a broken form means lost customers you never even hear about. If the customer gets an automatic reply, read that too. Does it show the right company name, the right phone number and a realistic time for an answer?

## 2. The phone number and the email

Tap the phone number on your site from your own phone. Does it dial the right number? Did you change your number without updating the website? Send a message to the email address shown on the site as well. Does it arrive, and does somebody actually read that mailbox?

## 3. Prices and opening hours

Outdated prices and wrong opening hours lead to unpleasant conversations. Check your Google Business Profile as well. Pay special attention before holidays such as Christmas, New Year, Easter and the summer break, and put the holiday hours on the site and in Google a few days early.

## 4. Speed

Run your site through pagespeed.web.dev. If the result has dropped sharply since last month, something has changed, perhaps a new plugin, a large photo or a script. Write the result into a simple table every month, and you will see at once when something gets worse.

## 5. Updates, if the site runs on WordPress

Are there waiting updates for WordPress, the theme or the plugins? Outdated plugins are the most common way small sites get hacked. Make a backup before updating. Also check for plugins you no longer use. A switched off plugin can still be dangerous if it is old, so delete it if you do not need it.

## 6. The backup

Do you know where the latest copy of the site is and how old it is? If the site disappeared tomorrow, how long would it take to bring it back? A good backup answers three questions. Is it automatic, because if it depends on someone remembering, one day they will forget? Where is it kept, because if it sits on the same server as the site and the server fails, you lose both? And have you ever tried to restore it, because a backup that will not open is not a backup?

## 7. The certificate, or padlock

Click the padlock next to the address and see until when the certificate is valid. If it expires in less than two weeks and normally renews itself, check that the renewal works.

## 8. Google Search Console

If you have Search Console, check whether there are new errors or pages Google cannot open. Look at page indexing, to see which pages Google has not added and why. Look at performance, to see whether impressions or clicks fall sharply. And look at the security and manual actions section, where Google tells you if the site is hacked or penalised. It should be empty.

## 9. The Google Business Profile

Are there new reviews you have not answered, or new questions from customers? Has someone suggested a change to your details? Google sometimes accepts changes suggested by users without asking you.

## 10. The domain

When does the domain expire? Is automatic renewal on? Is the card used for payment still valid?

## 11. The links on the site

Go quickly through the main pages and press the buttons. Do any lead to a page not found? Pay special attention to links to social media, to the map and to outside sites, which may have changed.

## A summary you can copy

Each month confirm that a test enquiry arrived, that the phone and email work, that prices and hours are right on the site and in Google, that you recorded the PageSpeed result, that no updates are waiting, that a recent backup exists, that the certificate is valid for more than two weeks, that Search Console shows no new errors, that you answered new reviews and that automatic domain renewal is on.

## If you find a problem

Do not panic. Most problems caught early are fixed quickly. Problems become expensive when they sit for months unnoticed. A broken form for three months means three months of lost enquiries.
