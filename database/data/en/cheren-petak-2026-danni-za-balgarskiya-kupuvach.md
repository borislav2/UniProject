Black Friday 2026 in Bulgaria and What the Data Shows
black-friday-2026-bulgarian-shopper-data
More cautious shoppers, fewer categories and a lot of price comparing because of the euro. What it means for a small online shop.
---
At eCommerce Annual Summit 2026, Market LINKS presented fresh data on how Bulgarians shop online and how they approach Black Friday. The picture is clear. People are online more than ever, but they spend more carefully.

## What the numbers say

According to the survey, 91 percent of adults use the internet, which is 2 points more than in 2025. The oldest generation is catching up fastest, with 79 percent of people over 62 now online. Of internet users, 54 percent shopped online in the last six months. That share has stayed at similar levels for three years in a row, so the market is not growing by itself.

Half of households (50 percent) report that their financial situation has worsened, compared with 42 percent in the previous two years. Of online shoppers, 72 percent plan a purchase on Black Friday, down from 78 percent last year. The planned budget is 528 euro per person, about 16 percent less than in 2025. Almost all of the drop comes from people buying in fewer categories, 2.6 on average instead of 2.9, and not from lower amounts.

## What these numbers mean in practice

**The market does not grow by itself.** When the share of online shoppers stays in one place for three years, every new customer is taken from some other shop. The winner is not the one who is simply online, but the one where it is easier, clearer and more reliable to buy.

**Older shoppers are arriving.** With 79 percent of people over 62 already online, these are customers who value a large font, clear buttons, a phone number and cash on delivery. A site that is comfortable for them is comfortable for everyone.

**People do not spend less per purchase, they buy in fewer categories.** This matters. The customer is not looking for the cheapest of everything. They choose where to spend, and there they are ready to pay. If you are in their two or three categories, you have to be the most convincing option in them.

## The euro effect

About 8 in 10 of those who plan to shop on Black Friday say that because of the euro they will spend more cautiously (79 percent), compare prices more carefully (78 percent) and check whether discounts are real (78 percent). In addition, 63 percent are open to buying from shops in other eurozone countries. Your competition is no longer only the Bulgarian shop across the street.

### What checking means

In practice the customer will open your shop and two or three others in neighbouring tabs. They will look at the price, the delivery, the time and the return terms. They will check in Google whether your price was the same a month ago. If there is any doubt, they will close the tab.

The advantage of a Bulgarian shop over foreign ones lies in what they find hard to offer. That means fast delivery with Econt or Speedy, cash on delivery, service in Bulgarian and easy returns inside the country. Show these things clearly and do not leave them buried in the terms and conditions.

## What a small business should do

First, show that the discount is real. Put the previous price and how much the customer saves, clearly and honestly. People will check, and an inflated discount costs trust for years. When you announce a discount in the EU you must in any case state the lowest price of the last 30 days.

Second, give prices in euro without surprises. The final price, the delivery cost and the delivery time should be visible on the product page and not only in the basket.

Third, start earlier. TikTok data for Europe shows that people begin looking for Black Friday offers as early as October. An email list launched now is the cheapest channel in November.

Fourth, focus on a few strong offers. Shoppers narrow their categories, so three or four truly good offers beat everything at ten percent off.

Fifth, make the site quick and clear on a phone. A large share of Black Friday purchases happen on a phone, often in a hurry. If the page loads slowly or the order form is complicated, the customer simply opens the next shop.

## A plan by weeks

In October, prepare. Choose three or four products or bundles that will be your main offers, check stock and supplier delivery times, and add an email field to the site saying "Write to me when the Black Friday offers start". Run the site through pagespeed.web.dev for phones and fix the heaviest photos.

At the start of November, warm up. Send subscribers a short email about what is coming and when. Prepare the offers page, still hidden or marked "Coming soon". Test the order from a phone from start to finish, covering the basket, delivery, payment and confirmation.

In Black Friday week, release the offers at the announced hour and email your subscribers. Watch stock and remove sold out products at once. Answer questions quickly by phone and chat, because in this week a slow answer is a lost order.

Afterwards, thank customers with an email after delivery and ask them for a review. Record which offers worked and which did not, because that is the most valuable information for next year.

## What not to do

Do not raise prices in October in order to cut them in November. People check, and the law requires you to show the lowest price of the last 30 days. Do not promise a delivery you cannot fulfil. In the peak week couriers are busy, so it is better to say honestly three to five working days than tomorrow and then be late. And do not email your whole list every day. A few well planned emails work better than a stream that makes people unsubscribe right before the holidays.
