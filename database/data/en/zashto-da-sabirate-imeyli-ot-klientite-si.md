Why Collect Customer Emails and How to Do It Without Spam

Profiles and ads are not yours, but an email list is. Here is how to build one honestly and legally without annoying people.
---
Customers who already trust you are the easiest ones to win back. The problem is that after the first purchase you often have no way to remind them you exist. An email list solves exactly that, and it is **yours**, because no algorithm decides who will see your message.

## Why email and not only social media

On Facebook and Instagram only a small share of your followers see a post unless you pay for ads. The rules change, profiles get blocked, and if the platform changes something tomorrow, you lose access to people you spent years gathering.

Email goes straight to the person's inbox. You can move the list from one program to another whenever you like, and you depend on nobody's decision about who sees your message. That does not make social media useless. It is sensible to let the people who like you there slowly become subscribers too.

## When it makes sense

It works for almost any local business, as long as you have a reason to write that is useful to the customer and not only to you. A salon or a clinic can remind people that the next treatment or check-up is due. A car service can remind them about an oil change or the annual inspection. A shop can announce a new product or a seasonal offer. A restaurant can share a new menu or free tables for an event. An accountant or a lawyer can warn clients about deadlines and changes in the law that affect them. A gym or a studio can send a new timetable or a new group.

## How to collect emails honestly

Ask with a clear reason. "Would you like us to remind you when it is time for your next check-up?" works better than "Subscribe to our newsletter".

Under GDPR the person must tick a box themselves to say they want to receive messages, and the box cannot be ticked in advance. Say what they will receive and how often, because "about one email a month" is reassuring. And make unsubscribing easy, with a link in every email. That is a legal requirement and a sign of respect.

You can ask in several places. On your website, add a separate checkbox to the enquiry form that reads "I would like to receive reminders and offers by email". In person, ask at payment or when booking the next appointment, briefly and out loud, and note it in your list. In the email after a purchase or service you can ask too, but if the person only gave their address for an invoice, you must ask for consent separately and cannot simply add them to the list. On the website you can also add a short form at the bottom of the pages, or a gentle pop up with a useful offer such as a guide, a first visit discount or a seasonal calendar.

## What you must not do

Do not buy email lists. That is illegal under GDPR, and your messages will land in spam anyway because people do not know you. Do not add everyone who wrote to you once for a quote, because an enquiry is not consent to marketing emails. And do not hide the unsubscribe link or ask for a password before someone can leave.

## How often to write

Rarely, but with a reason. One useful email a month is better than three promotions a week. People do not unsubscribe because you write, they unsubscribe because you write for no reason. A good rule is that for every three useful messages, such as advice, a reminder or news, one purely commercial message, such as an offer or a new product, is fine.

## Which program to use

You do not need anything complicated at the start. Programs such as Mailchimp, Brevo or MailerLite have free plans for small lists and ready made templates. What matters is that they handle unsubscribing automatically and keep a record of who agreed and when. You should be able to send from an address on your own domain, such as info@yourcompany.bg, and not from a free mailbox. The domain must also be set up correctly with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, so that your messages do not end up in spam.

## Where to start

Add a consent checkbox to the enquiry form on your website and ask the next ten customers in person. A list of a few dozen people who know you is worth more than thousands of random addresses. Then think about what to write first. A short welcome email is a good beginning, followed by reminders when it is time for the next service.

## Common questions

**How big must the list be to make sense?** There is no minimum. If you have 40 people who have been to you and want reminders, one email can bring back a few customers, and that is already worth it.

**Can I write to customers who gave me their email for an invoice?** Only about things related to their purchase. For marketing emails you need separate consent. The easiest way is to ask in the next message about their purchase.
