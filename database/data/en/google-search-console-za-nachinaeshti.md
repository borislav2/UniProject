Google Search Console for Beginners and Three First Checks
google-search-console-for-beginners
Google Search Console is free and shows how Google sees your site. Here are the three things to look at first, even if you are not technical.
---
Google Search Console is a free Google service that shows how the search engine sees your website. The name sounds complicated, and many owners open it once, see hundreds of numbers and charts, and never come back. That is a pity, because inside there are a few things that are easy to read and really useful.

You do not need to understand everything. It is enough to know where to look and what to think when you see something unusual.

## First the site is connected

To use Search Console you have to confirm that the site is yours. Google offers several ways to do this, and most often the person who built your site can do it in a few minutes. If you do not have access to it, ask them to add you as an owner or user.

Once it is connected, the data does not appear at once. In the first days you may see little or nothing, and then the information builds up gradually. This is normal and does not mean something is wrong.

## The first thing is queries and clicks

In the search results section you will see how many times your site was shown in Google and how many times someone clicked on it. A little lower there is a list of queries, which are the words people typed when they saw you.

This is the most useful part for a business owner. An accounting firm, for example, may see that people find it by "annual tax return" and not by "accounting services" as it had assumed. That is how you learn how customers really think, and you can answer them in their own language on your pages.

Do not be scared of small numbers. For a small local business it is quite normal to have only tens or a few hundred impressions. The direction over time matters more, and whether you see queries that make sense for your work.

## The second thing is pages Google cannot read

Search Console has a section that shows which pages are in the search engine and which are not. Sometimes you will see messages that something is not indexed, and that can look alarming. Not every such message is a problem. Some pages are deliberately not meant for search, for example the page shown after a form is sent.

Your task is to look closely at the pages that matter to you. If the page for your main service is on the list of pages that are not indexed, it is worth asking the person who looks after the site why. Sometimes the reason is an accidental setting that is easy to fix.

## The third thing is problem messages

Search Console sends an email when it finds something that deserves attention. It may be a problem with security, with the mobile version or with the speed of the site. Many people ignore these messages because they look technical.

Do not delete them. Forward them to the person who maintains your site and ask what they mean and whether they need action. That is how problems are caught before customers notice them. If you have no such person, that is a good signal that you may need [maintenance and hosting](/en/services/maintenance-and-hosting).

## What to do when you see something strange

Sometimes queries or impressions suddenly fall, and the first thought is that the site is broken. Before you worry, check whether there was a recent change to the site, or holidays, or a change of season. A car service naturally has fewer searches in August, and a guest house in Bansko is searched most before winter.

If the change cannot be explained by the season, write down the date and show it to the person who looks after the site. It will be much easier for them to find the cause than if you simply say something is wrong.

## How often to look

You do not need to log in every day. Once a month for fifteen minutes is quite enough for most small businesses. Look at whether impressions are rising or falling, which queries are new and whether there are emails about problems.

Do not draw hasty conclusions from one bad day. The numbers in a search engine fluctuate, sometimes for no visible reason. Follow the trend over several weeks.

This service shows how you stand in Google, but it says nothing about assistants such as ChatGPT and does not show your Google Maps profile. It also does not show how many visitors became customers. For those things there are other places to look.
