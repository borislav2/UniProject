How ChatGPT Chooses Which Businesses to Recommend
how-chatgpt-chooses-which-businesses-to-recommend
More people ask an assistant instead of searching in Google. Here is how such assistants arrive at one business and what you can do.
---
Imagine a person in Plovdiv writing to ChatGPT that they need a dentist who works on Saturdays and treats children. The assistant does not answer with ten blue links the way Google does. It gives one or two specific answers, usually with a short explanation of why. If your business is not among them, the customer may never find out that you exist.

This is a new situation, and it is normal that it is not clear how it works. Nobody outside knows the exact algorithm of these systems, and anyone selling you a sure formula is exaggerating. There are, however, things that are known well enough to act sensibly.

## Where the assistant gets its answers

Assistants such as ChatGPT and Gemini rely on two things. The first is what they learned during training from a huge amount of text. The second is what they can search for on the internet at the moment of the question, when they have that ability. In both cases they work with information that is publicly available and can be read.

That means something simple and important. If there is almost nothing written about your business in plain language in places that can be reached, the assistant has nothing to work with. It cannot recommend something it does not know about.

## A clear description beats a pretty design

A person looks at colours and photos. An assistant reads text. So a site that looks wonderful but says only "Welcome, quality and professionalism" gives it very little. It does not understand what you do, for whom, in which city and on what terms.

It is far more useful to say clearly, for example, that you are an accounting firm in Varna that keeps the books of small companies and the self employed, how many of you there are and what services you offer. Simple, concrete sentences that a person and a machine would both understand. The same goes for the services page and the frequently asked questions.

## Agreement between different places

An assistant rarely trusts a single source. If your site says one thing, your Google Maps profile another, and Facebook shows a third set of opening hours and a different phone number, that creates uncertainty. The system has no way of knowing which is true.

Check that the name, address, phone and opening hours are the same everywhere your business is mentioned. It sounds boring, but it is one of the most useful and cheapest things you can do.

## Reviews and mentions by other people

When others write about you, it weighs more than anything you say yourself. Real reviews in Google, a mention in a local directory, an article on a regional site or a recommendation from a trade body all show that the business really exists and works. Assistants, like people, pay attention to such traces.

Do not buy fake reviews. Besides being dishonest, they are often recognised and can hurt you in front of customers. The best way is simply to ask your happy customers to share their impressions when the service has gone well.

## Why different assistants answer differently

If you ask the same question to two assistants, you will often get different names. They do not use the same sources and are not trained in the same way. So it makes no sense to chase a position in one particular assistant, the way people once chased first place in Google.

It is more sensible to make sure the information about you is good, clear and found in several places. Then whichever assistant it is, it has something to work with. People's questions also differ. One asks for the cheapest option, another for the nearest, a third for the best reviewed, and each answer may point to a different business.

## What you can really do

Start with your own site and write on it the way you would explain to a new acquaintance what you do. Put your Google profile in order and keep it up to date. Check that your business details match everywhere. Collect honest reviews. Add a page of questions and answers that responds to what customers really ask on the phone.

None of these things guarantees that ChatGPT will recommend you. Nobody can promise that. But they raise the chance of being found and understood, and the same steps help in Google and with the customers who read your site.
