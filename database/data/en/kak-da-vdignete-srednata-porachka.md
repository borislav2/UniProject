How to Raise Your Average Order Instead of Paying More for Ads
raise-average-order-value-online-shop
When advertising gets pricier, it pays to have every customer buy a little more. Three ways to do it and one important number to watch.
---
Advertising gets more expensive every year, and every first sale costs more and more. At eCommerce Annual Summit 2026, Alexander Ivanov of CreateX showed a simple calculation. Sometimes it is more profitable to increase the value of each order than to buy more customers.

## First, watch the till and not the platform

Every advertising platform claims the sale for itself. A customer sees an ad on Facebook, searches the brand in Google and buys from an email. The three channels report the same order three times.

That is why you should look at the **overall result**, which is your total revenue divided by your total marketing spend. In the example from the talk, the channels report 65,000 euro of sales in total, while only 45,000 euro actually reaches the till. The Meta report shows a return of 4.0, while the real one is 2.25.

### How to calculate it each month

Take the **real revenue** of the shop for the month, from the till or the order system. Add up **all marketing costs**, meaning ads on every platform, the email program, agencies and influencers. Then divide the first by the second.

If the number grows, marketing as a whole works better, whatever each platform shows separately. If it falls, something is wrong, even if all the reports look green.

## The average order calculation

Take the same 10,000 euro of advertising and the same 500 orders. With an average order of 60 euro, revenue is 30,000 euro. With an average order of 85 euro, revenue is 42,500 euro. Or put another way, the same 30,000 euro of revenue with about 29 percent less advertising.

The average order is easy to work out, because it is the revenue for the period divided by the number of orders. Most shop platforms show it directly.

## Three ways to raise it

**One, three levels of offer on the product page.** For example one item, two items with a gift marked as best seller, and three items with free delivery. The middle option marked as best seller often wins.

**Two, a bar towards free delivery in the basket.** "Only 8 euro more to free delivery" nudges people to add something, especially if a suitable small product appears right beneath it.

**Three, a suggestion in the basket.** One or two products that go with the chosen one, with a clear price and an Add button.

### How to choose the free delivery threshold

It is good for the threshold to sit a little above your current average order, so that some customers add something to reach it. If the average order is 40 euro, a threshold of 50 euro is achievable. A threshold of 100 euro is too far, and people just pay for delivery.

### What to suggest in the basket

Suggest things that go together, such as a cream and a serum, coffee and filters, or food and a treat for the dog. Keep the suggestion small and cheap, significantly cheaper than the main product, otherwise the person starts to reconsider. And make it truly suitable. A suggestion of winter tyres for summer wheels irritates, so fewer but accurate suggestions are better.

## Bundles instead of discounts

Instead of 15 percent off everything, think about bundles, such as a dry skin set with a cleansing gel, a cream and a serum. The customer gets a ready solution, you sell more products at once, and you do not teach people to wait for sales. Bundles work especially well as gifts around the holidays.

## What if you sell something that runs out

Coffee, cosmetics, supplements and pet food are the cases where a subscription turns a one time purchase into steady income. Boris Bivolarski showed at the same conference that subscriptions work when the customer has full control, with easy pausing, a change of product and a reminder two or three days before payment. A hidden cancellation is the fastest way to lose trust.

Even if you have no technical subscription, a refill email sent at the moment the product usually runs out achieves part of the same effect.

## What not to do

Do not push. Three pop ups with offers before payment irritate people and make them abandon the cart. Do not hide the price of the add on or add it automatically, because that is both illegal and damaging to trust. And do not watch only the average order. If it rises but the number of orders falls, you may have overdone it, so watch both together.

## Where to start

Look at your average order for the last three months and put it next to your advertising spend. Then pick **one** of the changes above, run it for a month and compare. Do not change everything at once, or you will not know what worked.
