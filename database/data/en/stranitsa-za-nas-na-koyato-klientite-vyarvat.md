An How to Write an About Page That Customers Trust
about-page-customers-trust
The About page is often among the most read on a site. Here is what to write on it so a visitor feels safe to contact you.
---
Many owners write the About page last and in a hurry. They put in two general sentences about quality and experience and consider the job done. Yet a person deciding whether to trust you with their car, their teeth or their accounts very often goes straight there to find out who stands behind the business.

This is the place where a stranger decides whether you are people who can be trusted. That is why it deserves more attention than it usually gets.

## Say who you are, with names and faces

The biggest difference between trust and distrust is whether it is visible that real people stand behind the site. Write your names and add a photo in which you really look like yourself. It does not have to be a studio photo. One clear picture taken in your own premises is more convincing than a beautiful stock photo of smiling strangers.

If you are a team, introduce each person with a sentence or two about what they do and what they like about the work. That way the visitor sees that they will talk to a person and not to an anonymous company.

## Tell where you started

People love stories, as long as they are true and short. It does not have to be dramatic. A car service in Burgas, for example, can say that the mechanic started in a garage fifteen years ago and today works with the same people he started with. A paragraph like that shows stability much better than the word reliability.

Write only what is true and what you can prove. If you started three years ago, say three years. A modest but accurate story is received better than an exaggerated one.

## Explain how you work and why

The visitor wants to understand what they will experience if they contact you. Describe how working with you goes step by step, in ordinary sentences. When you get in touch with the customer, how you give a price, how long it takes and what happens if something does not work out.

It is good to share how you differ from others as well, but without pointing a finger at them. Perhaps you always explain to the customer what was done. Perhaps you do not start work until you have agreed the exact price. Things like that make an impression because they are concrete.

## Show proof that exists

If you have real reviews, quote them with the authors' permission. If you belong to a trade organisation or hold a licence, say so. If you have certificates or training related to your work, mention them briefly.

Do not invent awards, a number of customers or ratings that you do not have. It is easier to check than you think, and lost trust is hard to repair. When you do not have much to show, it is better to show a little that is real than to try to look big.

A person who has read the page and decided to trust you should not have to look for where to find you. Put a phone number, an address and an enquiry button at the end. If you have a physical office or site, show how to get there. Small details, such as where there is parking or which floor you are on, save hesitation. That way the person who has reached the end of the page does not have to go back and search, and simply calls you.

## Do not hide what is awkward

Small honest details often win more trust than smooth phrases. If you are a small team of three, say so. If you do not take certain kinds of jobs, explain why. A customer who reads that you do not deal with something you are not good at will believe the rest more easily. There is a practical benefit too, because the right people contact you and you do not waste time on conversations that would lead nowhere.

## The tone matters

Write the way you talk to a customer sitting across from you. Avoid formal office language and phrases that sound as if they came from an advertising brochure. Short, clear sentences are easier to read and look more honest.

Read what you have written out loud. If you stumble somewhere or feel awkward, the reader will probably feel it too. Give the text to someone who does not know you and ask what they understood about the business. The most useful corrections often come from exactly such a reading.
