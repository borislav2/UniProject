What to Ask an SEO Agency Before You Hire One
what-to-ask-an-seo-agency-before-hiring
Before you hire an SEO agency, ask a few simple questions. That way you learn what you get and nobody promises the impossible.
---
The manager of a dental clinic in Varna receives a message from an unknown company that promises to put her in the top results of Google. The price is low, the deadline is short and the contract has two pages. Before she answers, she does not know exactly what to ask. That fear of the unknown is quite understandable, because SEO sounds complicated and its results are not seen at once. The good news is that with a few simple questions you can tell a serious partner from one who simply sells hope.

We will also say something in defence of the people in the trade. Most agencies and specialists work honestly and do useful work. The problem is usually not ill will but unclear expectations between the two sides. So the questions below are not a trap, but a way for you and the agency to be on the same page before you begin.

## Ask what exactly will be done

A good answer is concrete. The agency should explain whether it will work on the technical state of the site, on the texts, on the Google profile, on links from other places or on all of these together. If you hear only general words such as optimisation and improving positions, ask them to explain what will be different on the site after the first month.

Ask also what is expected of you. Success often depends on things in your hands, such as photos, information about services or access to the site and your profile. If the agency says nothing about your part, it may mean it has not thought through how you will work together.

## What they can and cannot promise

No agency controls Google. The search engine decides by itself where to show a page, based on many things that nobody knows completely. So a promise of a guaranteed first place by a set date is not something a serious specialist would give. An honest answer sounds more like this. We will do this and that, we will watch what happens and we will adjust.

That does not mean you should not ask for measurable goals. You can agree, for example, that the agency tracks how many people come from search, how many enquiries you get and which pages attract interest. That way you will see together whether the work gives some direction, and nobody will lie to you about guarantees.

## Who will work on the project and how you will see it

Ask who will deal with your site. Sometimes sales are handled by one person and the work is done by another, and that is not a problem as long as you know whom to turn to. It matters more that there is a clear contact and a clear way of reporting.

Ask them to explain what report you will get, how often and in what language. A good report is understandable to a person without technical knowledge and shows what was done, what changed and what is coming. A report full of expensive words and charts but without a clear meaning does not help you.

## Whose site and whose data

A very important question that is often missed is in whose name the profiles and access will be. The Google profile, Search Console and the analysis of visits should be yours, and the agency should get access as a helper. That way, if one day you decide to change partner, nothing is lost and you do not have to start again.

Ask also what happens on termination. How long is the notice, is there a minimum term and do you get all the materials back? These things should be written into the contract and not promised in words.

## How to tell whether the work makes sense

You do not need to understand the technical details to judge the agency's attitude. If it asks you questions about your business, your customers, which services you want to sell and where, it is probably thinking about you and not only about a list of keywords. If it offers the same package to everyone, whether you are a dentist or a furniture shop, question how deeply it has looked into your case.

It is good to ask them to explain something in simple words. A specialist who can explain why they propose a certain action usually knows what they are doing.

## What to do before you sign

Write down what you want to achieve, for example more calls from a certain city, and share it with two or three candidates. Compare not only the prices but also how clearly they answer the questions above. Do not hurry if you are being pressed to decide at once.
