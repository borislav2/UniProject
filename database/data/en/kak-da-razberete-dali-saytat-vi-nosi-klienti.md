How to Tell If Your Website Brings You Customers
how-to-tell-if-your-website-brings-customers
No complicated reports needed. Here are four simple ways to find out whether your website works for the business or just exists.
---
"We have a website, but I do not know whether it brings anything." We hear this often. You do not need complex reports to find out. Here are four simple ways, and then how to arrange the numbers so they actually tell you something.

## 1. Ask every new customer

"How did you hear about us?" It sounds old fashioned, but it is the most accurate source of information. Write the answers in a notebook or a table, and after a month you will see the picture.

A few tips make the answers useful. Ask in the same way every time. Record the answers with the same words, such as Google, Google Maps, website, recommendation, Facebook, AI assistant or passed by. And clarify, because if they say the internet, ask whether it was Google or Facebook. In the enquiry form on your site you can also add an optional field that asks where they heard about you, with a few options.

## 2. Count the enquiries from the form

Every enquiry from the website is a precisely measurable result. How many arrive each month? Are there more or fewer than last year? If there are none, either the form does not work or nobody reaches it.

Count the **phone calls from the site** as well. If the phone number on the site differs from the one in your Google profile, for example a second number, you will know exactly how many calls come from the site. If it is the same, ask during the call.

## 3. Look at your Google Business Profile

In your profile you see how many people called you, asked for directions or opened your website directly from Google. It is free and very clear statistics. Compare month with month and not day with day. If your business is seasonal, compare with the same month last year, because July and December look different for a restaurant and for an air conditioning company.

## 4. Google Search Console

This is a free tool from Google that shows which searches your site appears for, how many times it was shown and how many times people clicked. If the site hardly shows at all, the problem is visibility. If it shows but nobody clicks, the problem is in the titles and descriptions.

### How to read Search Console

In the performance section there are four numbers. Clicks are how many times people opened your site from Google. Impressions are how many times the site appeared in the results. CTR is the percentage of impressions that became clicks. Average position is the average place in the results.

The most useful part is the queries section further down, which shows the exact words people use to find you. If there are only words containing your company name, people who already know you are finding you. New customers come when you begin to appear for words such as car service Burgas or beauty salon Lozenets.

## 5. Google Analytics, if you have it

Analytics shows how many people visit the site, where they come from and what they do. A small business does not need dozens of reports, because three questions are enough. How many visits do you have each month, and are they growing? Where do they come from, whether Google, direct visits, social media, AI assistants such as ChatGPT or referrals from other sites? And how many of them reach the form or tap the phone number? This last one has to be set up as an event or a conversion, otherwise Analytics does not know about it.

## What the results mean

If there are visits but no enquiries, the site does not convince people or the form is hard to use, so work on the content and the form. If there are no visits, people do not find you, so work on the Google profile and visibility. If there are enquiries but not from the right people, the text attracts the wrong audience, so be more specific about what you do and for whom. If there are enquiries but few become customers, the problem may not be the site at all, and could be the price, the speed of your reply or the phone conversation.

## A simple monthly table

You need nothing more than this, filled in once a month. Write the month, the enquiries from the form, the calls from Google, the clicks from Search Console and the new customers from the site. After three or four months you will see whether the direction is up and which number is moving.

## How much a site should bring

It depends on the business. A useful calculation is to ask how much one new customer is worth to you over a year. If it is 300 euro and the site brings two new customers a month, it brings you 7,200 euro a year. Compare that with what you pay for it. The sum often surprises in both directions.

## Common questions

**How long after launch can I expect results?** Enquiries from people who already know you come at once. Enquiries from new people who find you in Google usually begin to grow after several weeks to several months, depending on competition and how much you work on visibility.

**Do I need paid tools?** No. Search Console, Google Business Profile and Analytics are free. Paid tools make sense when you have a specific question the free ones cannot answer.
