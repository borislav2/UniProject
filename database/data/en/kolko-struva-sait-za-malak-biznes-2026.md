The price of a website depends on a few clear things. Here is how to spot them so you can compare quotes sensibly and not pay for extras.
---
When the owner of a small company asks how much a website costs, almost every answer begins with "it depends". It sounds like a way to dodge the question, but it is honest. Two sites that look the same at first glance can need very different amounts of work, and the work is what sets the price.

That is why it is more useful to understand what stands behind a number than to hear one figure. Then you can read quotes carefully and see where you are paying for something needed and where for something your business will never use.

## First you ask what the site must do

A beauty salon in Varna that only needs to show its services, prices, address and a call button has a very different task from a car service in Burgas that wants customers to book a time online. The first site is fairly simple. The second needs a booking system that connects to the workshop's schedule and sends confirmations.

The more actions you expect from a visitor, the more work there is behind the scenes. An enquiry form is a small task. Online payment, customer registration or a calendar of free slots are considerably bigger.

## How many pages and how much text there is

A site of five pages is built faster than one of forty. But the number is not everything. It matters who writes the texts and who prepares the photos. If you provide ready, clear texts and good photos yourself, the work speeds up. If you expect the team to invent the texts, write them and organise a photo shoot, that is extra hours of work by several different people.

Many quotes look cheap precisely because they assume that the texts and photos are already done. Then it turns out that this is the hardest part and it has been left to you.

## A design made for you or a ready template

A ready template is like off the peg clothing. It suits many people, costs less and is ready quickly. A custom design is like a tailored suit, because it is made specially for your business, your colours and your way of working. It takes more time and therefore costs more.

Neither option is better in principle. A lawyer or a dentist often benefits from a design that conveys seriousness and trust. A small family guest house in Bansko can do very well with a well set up template, as long as the photos of the house are good.

## What you cannot see but still pay for

Part of the price goes to things the visitor does not notice but which decide whether the site works well. This includes loading speed, correct display on a phone, the underlying structure that helps Google understand what a page is about, and the security of the connection.

On top of that come the domain and the hosting, which are almost always paid every year separately from the build. Ask explicitly what is included, how it renews and who owns the domain. The domain should be in your name and not in the name of the contractor.

## Maintenance after launch

A website is not something you make once and forget. Systems are updated, certificates expire, and sometimes something breaks after a change in the browsers. If nobody watches this, one day the site simply stops working and you find out from a customer.

So with every quote it is good to ask what happens after launch, who is responsible when there is a problem and how long the reply takes. Some people prefer to pay monthly and know that someone looks after the site, and others prefer a one time price and take the risk themselves. Both approaches are legitimate, and what matters is that you know which one you are choosing.

## How to compare quotes

Ask for all quotes against the same list. Write which pages you want, which actions the site must have and who will provide the texts and photos. Then compare not the final sum but what exactly you get for it. A quote that is a third cheaper but does not include texts, hosting and maintenance often turns out more expensive after half a year.

Be careful with promises that nobody can guarantee. No honest contractor can promise you first place in Google or a certain number of customers. A site creates the conditions for people to find you and trust you, but the results also depend on many things outside it.
