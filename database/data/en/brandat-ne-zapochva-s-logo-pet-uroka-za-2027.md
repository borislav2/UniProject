Branding for a New Business Starts Before the Logo
branding-starts-before-the-logo-five-lessons
A successful brand begins with a gap in the market and a clear reason to be chosen. Five lessons from the founders of NinjaMame for a new business.
---
Many new businesses start with a logo, a website and an ad campaign. Alexander Ivanov and Dimitar Shikov of NinjaMame, part of Home Infinity, started elsewhere. At Digital Community Day 2026 they told how to launch a brand in 2027 and why the logo comes late.

Their thesis fits in one sentence. A successful brand does not begin with a logo, a website or an ad campaign, but with a clear reason that solves a specific problem.

## Lesson one is to start with a reason and not a product

Before you draw packaging, answer the question of which problem you solve and why people will choose you. The speakers say you need a reason for people to choose exactly this. If the reason is only that you make it nicer, the market will pass you by.

## Lesson two is to choose where to enter and not what to make

The first decision is not what to produce but where it makes sense to enter. A big market is not the same as a suitable market. On top of that you must put your own resources, which are money, time, skills and connections. A small team in a large category full of giants rarely wins.

## Lesson three is to look for a gap and not only a growing market

The choice rests on three things, which are a trend, a category and an opportunity. The key note is that a growing market on its own is not enough.

Their example is the snack. According to the speakers, there was no quality salty protein snack on the market. Their product was born from that gap, roasted edamame. It is not just another snack but an answer to a specific absence.

For a small business the question is the same on a smaller scale. What is missing in your town, in your circle of customers, in your service? A beauty salon that works late for people on shifts? A car service that sends a photo of the damage before it asks for money?

## Lesson four is to write the reason in one sentence

The speakers give a formula for positioning. We are this, for this audience, who want this result, because of this advantage. For example, we are a car service for family cars in Ruse, for people who want to know the price before the repair, because we first send a photo and a quote. If you cannot fill in the four blanks, you are not yet ready for a website and ads.

The second part of the lesson is to start from a niche and scale towards the mass consumer, not the other way round. A small group that loves you is a better base than a wide one that is indifferent.

## Lesson five is that marketing creates demand and distribution turns it into a purchase

Marketing creates demand, and distribution, meaning that the product is where a person will buy it, turns it into a purchase. NinjaMame use PR parcels, outdoor advertising and event activations to get the brand heard, and points of sale so that people can buy it.

For a service this translates simply. An ad or an article that creates interest is not enough. There must be an easy next move, such as a phone number in a visible place, a form or a time for a conversation. Demand without a path to purchase gets lost.

## Time is a decision too

Two numbers from the presentation, with no source given on the slide, are 18 months of development and more than 2,500 points of sale in less than two months after launch. The first is a reminder that a good idea has its moment and that preparation takes time. The second shows what happens when the idea, the market and the distribution are lined up beforehand. We do not read them as an expectation of a normal result.

## Five questions before you order a website or ads

The formula is useful even when you are not launching a new product. Here is an exercise you can do alone in half an hour.

1. Fill in the formula with its four blanks.
2. If you get a general sentence such as "for everyone who wants more customers", go back to the question of who exactly the customer is.
3. Write down which problem you solve and what the customer does if they do not solve it with you.
4. Write what is different about you, without the words quality and professional.
5. Check whether the customer can find you and buy easily, meaning where they search and where they see your phone number.

If any of the five has no answer, it is not yet time for a logo. It is better to clarify the foundation first.
