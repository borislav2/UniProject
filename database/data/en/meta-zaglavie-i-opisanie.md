Meta Title and Description That Get Clicks in Google
meta-title-and-description-guide
The blue line and the two lines of text under it decide whether people click on you or on your neighbour. Here is how to write them well.
---
When your site appears in Google, people see three things. A blue title, an address and two lines of text. These are the **meta title** and the **meta description**. They are not visible on the page itself, but they decide whether someone clicks.

Imagine you are on a shelf in a shop next to nine other products. The title and the description are your label. If the label does not say clearly what is inside, people take the one next to it.

## The meta title

Aim for about 50 to 60 characters. Google cuts anything longer and replaces the end with three dots. Begin with what people search for, for example "Roof repair in Plovdiv, Healthy Roof" and not "Home, Healthy Roof Ltd". Every page needs its own title. If every page is called "Home", Google does not know which one to show.

A formula that works is the service plus a city or a detail, followed by the business name. A dental practice might use "Dental clinic in Lozenets, Sofia" and then its name. A car service might use "Tyre change and balancing in Burgas". A bakery might use "Custom wedding cakes in Varna". For the contact page, "Contacts and opening hours" with the business name works well. For a single service, "Teeth whitening, price and duration" is a good start.

## The meta description

Aim for about 120 to 155 characters. Say what the person gets and what to do next, for example "Inspection and a written quote within 48 hours. We work in Plovdiv and the region. Call us or send a photo of the roof."

Write for people, not for Google. Stuffing in keywords does not help and puts readers off.

Include the concrete benefit, such as a quote within 48 hours, Saturday appointments or free delivery over a certain amount. Include the place, a city or district, if you are a local business. And end with the next step, such as call us, book a time or see the menu.

### A before and after example

Before, the description reads "Welcome to the website of our company. We are an industry leader with many years of experience and we offer quality services."

After, it reads "Roof repair and waterproofing in Plovdiv. Inspection and a written quote within 48 hours. Send a photo of your roof and we will call you."

The second one says what, where and what to do. The first could belong to any website.

## Google does not always use your text

Sometimes Google picks a passage from the page itself if it decides it answers the search better. That is normal. A well written description still shows more often and wins more clicks.

The same goes for the title. If it is too long, repetitive or does not match the content, Google may rewrite it. The more accurately it describes the page, the more likely yours stays.

## How to check yours

Type site, then a colon, then your domain into Google, written together without spaces. You will see every page Google knows with its title and description. Look for repeats, the word "Home", "Untitled", or text cut in the middle.

If you have **Google Search Console**, open the performance report and look for pages with many impressions but few clicks. These are the first candidates for a better title and description. Google already shows you, people just do not choose you.

## Where to change them

On WordPress you can use a plugin such as Yoast SEO or Rank Math, which adds fields for the SEO title and description under every page. On Shopify, Wix and similar platforms, the settings of each page have an SEO section. On a custom site it depends how it was built. On the sites we build, the title and description of every page can be changed from the admin panel without a programmer.

After a change it can take several days to weeks for Google to update the result.

## Common mistakes

The same description on every page is the most common one. A title with only the business name and nothing about what you do is another. Capital letters and many exclamation marks look cheap. And promises the page does not keep make people click, find nothing of what was promised and go back to Google.

## Common questions

**Do keywords in the description help my position?** Not directly. The description affects whether people click, not where you rank. But if the words the person searched for appear in it, Google makes them bold and your result stands out.

**Should I write separate descriptions for the English version?** Yes. Each language version needs its own titles and descriptions, written for people who search in that language and not machine translated.
