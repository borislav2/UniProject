What to Prepare Before You Order a Website for Your Business
what-to-prepare-before-you-order-a-website
A website gets built faster, better and cheaper when you know the answers to a few questions. Here is the full list to go through.
---
The biggest delay in building a website is rarely the building itself. It is usually the waiting for texts, photos and decisions. If you prepare these things in advance, the site comes out faster and better.

You do not need everything to be ready. But the more of this list you have, the shorter the conversations will be and the more accurate the quote.

## 1. What should the website do?

Should people call you? Send enquiries? Buy? See the menu? Pick **one main goal**. Everything on the site will lead towards it.

Think about how you will know that the site works. Five enquiries a month, people no longer phoning to ask the opening hours, or selling online outside your town are all fine examples. A concrete goal helps us decide what is important and what can wait.

## 2. Who are your customers?

Not everyone. Who is your typical customer, what are they looking for and what worries them? It could be a homeowner with an old roof, a mother looking for a children's dentist, or a company that needs catering.

Write down the **five questions customers ask most often** on the phone. They are ready made content for the questions page, and they show what should be visible on the site at once.

## 3. A list of services and prices

Even approximate ones. If you do not want to show prices, think at least about "from" a figure. For each main service note in a few words what it includes, who it is for, how long it takes and what the price depends on. Do not worry about how it sounds, because we shape the texts together.

## 4. Photos

Your own photos are always better than stock ones, whether of the team, the premises or your work. Phone photos are perfectly enough as long as they are bright and horizontal.

When you photograph with a phone, use daylight without a flash, near a window or outside. Hold the phone horizontally, because most photos on a site are wide. Keep the background clean, and remove boxes, cables and cups from the frame. Take several versions of everything, because it is easier to choose from ten than to shoot again. And make sure the people in the photos agree to appear on the site.

If your work is visual, such as renovations, cakes, haircuts or furniture, take **before and after** photos. They are the most convincing photos a site can have.

## 5. A logo

If you have a logo, find it in the best possible quality, ideally in a vector format such as SVG, AI or PDF. If you do not have one, tell us. If your logo is only a low quality picture or was made long ago, this is a good moment to refresh it. It does not have to be complicated. What matters is that it looks good both small and large.

## 6. Domain and access

Do you have a domain? In whose name is it? Do you know the password for the registrar? If you have an old site, who has access to it? Prepare access to your company email and where it is hosted, to your Google Business Profile, to Google Analytics and Search Console if they exist, and to the social networks the site will link to. If any of this is in the name of a former employee or an old agency, now is the moment to move it to yourself.

## 7. Sites you like

Pick two or three sites, from competitors or from completely different businesses, and note what exactly you like in them. It saves hours of explanation. Note what you do **not** like too. You may not want a dark site, photos of people who are not yours, or pop up windows that annoy you. Sometimes that says more than the likes.

## 8. Who decides

If the decisions are made by two partners, it is better for both to join the conversations from the start and not see the site for the first time at the end.

## 9. The legal side

Every site that collects data through a form needs a **privacy policy**. If it has tracking such as Google Analytics, it also needs a **cookie policy** and a consent banner. Prepare the company details, meaning the exact company name, registration number, registered office and address. If you sell online, you need terms and conditions as well.

## 10. Budget and deadline

Tell us what budget you have in mind and whether there is a date by which the site must be ready, such as an opening, a season or an exhibition. This is not so that we can take everything you have, but so that we can propose a solution that fits both. Our packages and prices are published on the [packages page](/en/packages).

## A short checklist

Have one main goal for the site. Describe your typical customer and the five most common questions. List your services with approximate prices. Collect your own photos, at least ten to fifteen. Find the logo in good quality. Gather access to the domain, email and Google profile. Choose two or three sites you like and note what you dislike. Prepare the company details for the legal pages. And decide on a budget and a preferred deadline.

## Common questions

**Do I have to write the texts myself?** No. We need the information, which means what you do, for whom, at what prices and what makes you different. You can tell it in a conversation or jot it down quickly. Shaping the text is part of our work.

**What happens to the old website?** We go through it together and decide what to keep. If the old pages have positions in Google, we set up redirects to the new ones so they are not lost.
