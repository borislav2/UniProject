Email Subject Lines That Get Opened, Advice for Small Business
email-subject-lines-that-get-opened
How to write an email subject line people open. Simple advice on length, tone and honesty for small business email campaigns.
---
The inbox of most people looks like a cluttered kitchen counter. Everything is piled up, nothing is arranged, and a person grabs only what seems important. Your email competes with dozens of other messages, and in that contest the subject line is almost the only thing the recipient sees before deciding.

## The subject line is a promise

The best subject line is an honest promise of what is inside. When people open an email and find something different from what was promised, they feel misled, and next time they will pass it even faster. So do not look for tricks, but say clearly what the reader gets.

Think of a car service in Burgas that reminds customers about the annual technical inspection. A subject such as "Your technical inspection is coming up" is clear and useful. A subject such as "Do not miss this!" says nothing, and it also looks like junk mail.

Before you send, ask yourself whether a person who reads only the subject understands what the email is about. If the answer is no, rewrite it.

## Length and order

Many mail apps, especially on phones, show only the beginning of the subject. So put the most important word in the first half. A subject of about four to nine words is usually read in full, while a longer one risks being cut exactly where the interesting part is.

Order matters too. Start with what concerns the recipient and leave the company name to the sender line, where it shows anyway. A subject that begins with "News from our team" wastes the most valuable words on something nobody cares about.

## The concrete beats the general

People open emails that concern them personally or solve a specific problem. A beauty studio in Varna can write "Open manicure appointments this week" instead of "A special offer just for you". The first subject says what, when and about what, while the second could be about anything.

Numbers and dates help when they are real. "We work until 24 December" is useful information. "Only 3 places" is allowed only if there really are three places. Invented urgency is noticed quickly and destroys trust, so do not use it.

The recipient's name in the subject sometimes works, but do not overdo it. If you write to everyone "John, we have an offer for you", the effect will be the opposite, because people soon recognise a mass email and stop believing it.

## What to avoid

Too many exclamation marks, words in capital letters and promises of profit send the email to the junk folder. Phrases such as FREE, YOU WIN or Last chance are well known to mail filters, and even if the email reaches the inbox, the reader recognises it as advertising.

Avoid subjects that pretend to be personal correspondence, such as "About our conversation", when there was no conversation. That is deception and most people feel it at once. An honest tone pays off more slowly but more surely, because it earns you readers who keep opening your emails years later. The sender matters too. If emails come in the name of a person, for example the owner, and not from a faceless address, recipients recognise and trust them more easily.

## How to find out which subject works

The best teacher is your own list, because no outside example knows your customers. Most sending programs let you try two different subjects on two small groups and see which one is opened more often. Do not be fooled by single results. If your list is small, watch several sends in a row and look for a repeating pattern and not a chance fluctuation.

Pay attention also to the preview text that sits next to the subject in the inbox. It is your second chance to persuade the reader, and many people leave it empty or with the text "If you cannot see this email, click here". Write one supporting sentence that continues the thought of the subject.

## A small first experiment this week

Next time you prepare an email, write five different subjects and choose the most honest one. Then record in an ordinary notebook which subject you sent, when and how many people opened the email. It does not need to be complicated, and a table with three columns is enough. After a few emails you will have your own observations, which are worth more than any outside advice.
